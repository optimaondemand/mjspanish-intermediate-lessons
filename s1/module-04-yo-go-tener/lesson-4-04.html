LESSON 4.04 · PUTTING IT TOGETHER
Meet Diego — and hear him introduce himself
Everything below is grammar you now own: yo-go verbs and tener expressions, in the present tense.
👋 BEFORE YOU READ

Diego is a student, just like you. In a few short sentences he tells you his age, how he feels, and some things he does. Read each line, then press ▶ to hear it in a native voice. Notice how many of this week's pieces show up: tengo, hago, salgo, digo, and the tener feelings.

📖 LECTURA · “ME PRESENTO” (I INTRODUCE MYSELF)
Me llamo Diego y tengo trece años. — My name is Diego and I am thirteen years old.
Después de la escuela, yo hago la tarea. — After school, I do the homework.
A las cuatro siempre tengo hambre. — At four o'clock I am always hungry.
Los sábados yo salgo con mi familia. — On Saturdays I go out with my family.
Cuando tengo sueño, no digo mucho. — When I'm sleepy, I don't say much.
Tengo ganas de aprender español este año. — I feel like learning Spanish this year.
🌎 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · "I HAVE HUNGER"

Here's a real window into how a language thinks. English treats hunger, thirst, cold, and fear as things you are: "I am hungry." Spanish treats them as things you have: "Tengo hambre." Age works the same way — a Spanish speaker doesn't say she is thirteen, she says she tiene trece años ("has thirteen years").

This isn't a small textbook rule — it's how tens of millions of people across the United States talk every day. Spanish is one of the most spoken languages in the country, and in Spanish-speaking homes a child learns early to say tengo hambre and tengo cinco años long before any grammar lesson. When you learn these tener expressions, you're not memorizing a quirk — you're learning to say true things about yourself the way half a billion people do.
