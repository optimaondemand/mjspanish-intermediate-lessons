LESSON 4.02 · THE PATTERN
Regular everywhere — except "yo," where you add -go
Learn the one twist, then meet tener, the verb you'll use every day this year.
👋 THE ONE IDEA
Seven common verbs are regular in almost every form. The only surprise is the yo ("I") form, where the stem gains a -go.

Meet the "yo-go" verbs: tener (to have), hacer (to do/make), poner (to put), salir (to leave / go out), traer (to bring), venir (to come), and decir (to say/tell). Their nickname comes from the yo form: every one ends in -go. Learn the yo forms as a little set, and the rest of each verb behaves like the regular endings you already know.

Say the yo column out loud like a chant: tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, vengo, digo. That gold -go is the whole trick. Notice hago (not "haco") and digo (not "dico") — the sound softens too, so trust the spelling in the chart.

🧩 THE FULL CHART · tener (to have)

Tener is worth learning completely — you will use it constantly. It is a yo-go verb (yo tengo), and it has one extra wrinkle: in the tú, usted/él/ella, and ustedes/ellos/ellas forms, the e of the stem becomes ie (tienes). The nosotros form stays regular.

Subject (who)Form of tener
yo (I)tengo
tú (you, friendly)tienes
usted / él / ella (you-formal / he / she)tiene
nosotros / nosotras (we)tenemos
ustedes / ellos / ellas (you-all / they)tienen

So: tengo / tienes / tiene / tenemos / tienen. Two of this week's verbs behave like this: venir also does e→ie (vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, vienen), and decir does e→i (digo, dices, dice, decimos, dicen). The other four (hacer, poner, salir, traer) are regular after the yo-go, so haces, pones, sales, traes look completely normal.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Watch the yo-go verbs at work with words you already know. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back:

Yo tengo dos hermanos. — I have two brothers. (tener → yo tengo)
Yo hago la tarea en casa. — I do the homework at home. (hacer → yo hago)
Yo salgo con mis amigos. — I go out with my friends. (salir → yo salgo)
Tú tienes un perro. — You have a dog. (tener → tú tienes, e→ie)
Nosotros venimos a la escuela. — We come to school. (venir → nosotros venimos, regular stem)

The yo form gets the -go; everything else uses endings you already own.

⚡ TRY IT · CONJUGATION DRILL
Pick the correct form. The subject is given — watch for the yo-go and the stem changes. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. Yo ____ la tarea por la noche. (hacer — I do the homework at night.)
2. Yo ____ los libros en la mesa. (poner — I put the books on the table.)
3. Yo ____ de la escuela a las tres. (salir — I leave school at three.)
4. Tú ____ dos gatos en casa. (tener — You have two cats at home.)
5. Yo ____ a clase temprano. (venir — I come to class early.)
6. Usted ____ la verdad. (decir — You [formal] tell the truth.)

🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

Seven verbs, one twist: the yo form ends in -go — tengo, hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, vengo, digo. Learn tener in full (tengo / tienes / tiene / tenemos / tienen), and remember that venir and decir also shift their stem outside the yo form.
