LESSON 4.01 · A NEW WEEK, A SMALL SECRET
Some of the most useful verbs break one little rule
Nothing to memorize today — just meet the pattern and see what you already own.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
A handful of everyday Spanish verbs are perfectly regular… except in the yo ("I") form, where they add a surprise -go.

You have spent three weeks building the regular verb engine: -AR verbs (-o / -as / -a / -amos / -an) and -ER / -IR verbs (-o / -es / -e / -emos / -en). This week we meet seven of the most common verbs in the language — tener, hacer, poner, salir, traer, venir, decir — and they are almost regular. The only twist is the yo form. Once you see it, you own it.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. You can already conjugate two whole families of verbs. Read these out loud — every one is yours:

Yo hablo — I speak Tú comes — you eat Nosotros vivimos — we live Ellos estudian — they study

The regular endings are already in your ear. This week's verbs use those same endings almost everywhere — so you are really only learning one new thing per verb. Let's warm up the regular pattern first.

⚡ WARM-UP · REGULAR VERBS YOU ALREADY KNOW
Quick review before the new stuff. Pick the correct form — instant feedback on each.
1. Yo ____ español en clase. (hablar — I speak Spanish in class.)
2. Nosotros ____ en la cafetería. (comer — We eat in the cafeteria.)
3. Tú ____ una carta. (escribir — You write a letter.)
👀 A FIRST LOOK AT THE TWIST

Here is the whole secret in one line. Watch the yo form:

tener → yo tengo hacer → yo hago salir → yo salgo

See the little -go that shows up in the yo form? Some teachers call these the "yo-go" verbs because of exactly that. Every other form stays close to normal. Tomorrow you learn the full pattern — today just notice the shape.
