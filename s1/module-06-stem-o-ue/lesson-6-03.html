LESSON 6.03 · NEW WORDS, KNOWN PATTERN
Seven boot verbs to make your own
You learned the o→ue engine yesterday — today we just load in the words that ride on it.
👋 WHY THIS IS THE EASY DAY

Yesterday you learned the o→ue boot. Today is pure vocabulary — seven common verbs that all follow that same boot (plus jugar, the u→ue one). No new grammar to wrestle with: once you know a verb is a boot verb, you already know how to say I ___, you ___, we ___. Listen to each one in a native voice, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

Each word is tagged with its stem change — o→ue or u→ue — so you can see the family at a glance.

🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · SEVEN BOOT VERBS

These are infinitives (the “to ___” form). Verbs don't carry gender, so there is no color-coding here — press play and say each one aloud.

  • poder o→ueto be able (to) / can
  • dormir o→ueto sleep
  • volver o→ueto return / to come back
  • almorzar o→ueto eat lunch / to have lunch
  • encontrar o→ueto find / to meet (up with)
  • costar o→ueto cost
  • jugar u→ueto play (a sport or game)
💡 TWO VERBS TO WATCH

costar (to cost) almost always shows up in just two forms — cuesta (it costs) and cuestan (they cost) — because it is usually things that cost money. And jugar is your one u→ue verb: juego, juegas, juega… It behaves like the o→ue family in every other way.

📝 PUT ONE TO WORK

Because these ride on yesterday's boot pattern, you can already build sentences with them:

  • Yo duermo ocho horas. — I sleep eight hours.
  • Tú juegas muy bien. — You play very well.
  • Nosotros volvemos a las tres. — We come back at three. (nosotros keeps the o!)

Same move as always: flip the stem o→ue (or u→ue), add the regular ending — and leave nosotros alone.
