LESSON 6.04 · BOOT VERBS IN THE WILD
A lazy Saturday, told with boot verbs
See the whole week's pattern working together in real sentences — then meet a Spanish word you already use in English.
👋 READ, THEN LISTEN

Here is a short paragraph about a relaxed Saturday. Every verb in bold is a boot verb from this week. Read each sentence first, then press ▶ to hear it in a native voice — reading and hearing together makes it stick. Watch how nosotros forms keep their original vowel.

📖 UN SÁBADO TRANQUILO · A QUIET SATURDAY
Los sábados, yo duermo hasta muy tarde. — On Saturdays, I sleep until very late.
Mi hermano juega al fútbol en el parque. — My brother plays soccer in the park.
A la una, nosotros almorzamos en familia. — At one o'clock, we eat lunch as a family. (nosotros keeps the o)
Después del almuerzo, mi abuelo duerme una siesta. — After lunch, my grandfather takes a nap.
Por la tarde, mis amigos y yo volvemos al parque y encontramos a otros amigos. — In the afternoon, my friends and I return to the park and meet other friends. (both nosotros forms keep the o)

Count them: duermo, juega, duerme flipped their vowel (inside the boot), while almorzamos, volvemos, encontramos kept theirs (the nosotros form, outside the boot).

🌍 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · SIESTA

Did you notice siesta in the reading? That is not just a Spanish word — it is an English word too. English borrowed siesta straight from Spanish, and we use it to mean a short afternoon rest, often after the midday meal. When you say “I need a siesta,” you are already speaking a little Spanish.

It pairs naturally with this week's verb dormir (to sleep): Mi abuelo duerme una siesta. Keep an ear out — borrowed Spanish words like this one are all over everyday English.
