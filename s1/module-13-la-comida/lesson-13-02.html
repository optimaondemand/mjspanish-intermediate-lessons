LESSON 13.02 · NO NEW GRAMMAR TODAY
Three verbs you already conjugate — now at the table
Everything here is review. We're just aiming familiar verbs at food.
👋 A QUICK, HONEST HEADS-UP
The grammar today is recycled. Your new work this week is vocabulary — not verb rules.

To talk about food, you'll lean on four things you already own: querer (to want), preferir (to prefer), pedir (to order / ask for), and me gusta(n) (I like). Three of them are “boot” (stem-changing) verbs you met earlier this year, and gustar is from last year. Today is a refresher so the forms are fresh in your ear — then the rest of the week is all about the words that go with them.

🧩 REMEMBER THE “BOOT”

In a boot verb, the stem vowel changes in every form except nosotros — the one form that sits outside the boot. Two patterns cover all three verbs:

e → ie: querer, preferir · e → i: pedir

So querer → quiero, preferir → prefiero, and pedir → pido. Same boots you already wore in Modules 5 and 7.

📝 THE THREE VERBS, SIDE BY SIDE (present tense)
Subject (who)querer (want)preferir (prefer)pedir (order)
yo (I)quieroprefieropido
tú (you, friendly)quieresprefierespides
usted / él / ellaquiereprefierepide
nosotros / nosotras (we)queremospreferimospedimos
ustedes / ellos / ellasquierenprefierenpiden

Notice the nosotros row keeps the plain vowel every time — queremos, preferimos, pedimos — because it sits outside the boot. And remember: for a group we use ustedes (the standard form across Latin America), never a -áis form.

💡 DON'T FORGET GUSTAR

Gustar matches the thing liked: one thing → me gusta (Me gusta la manzana — I like the apple); more than one → me gustan (Me gustan las naranjas — I like oranges). Nothing new — just watch singular vs. plural.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Same verbs you already know, now carrying food words. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back:

Yo quiero una manzana, por favor. — I want an apple, please. (querer → qu-ie-ro)
¿Prefieres frutas o verduras? — Do you prefer fruits or vegetables? (preferir → pref-ie-res)
Nosotros pedimos la cena en el restaurante. — We order dinner at the restaurant. (pedir → nosotros = pedimos)
A mí me gustan las naranjas. — I like oranges. (plural thing → gustan)

Every verb here is one you've conjugated before. The only thing that's new is the food.

⚡ TRY IT · PICK THE RIGHT FORM
The subject is given — choose the present-tense form that matches. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. Yo ____ una manzana. (querer — I want an apple.)
2. Nosotros ____ la cena a las ocho. (pedir — We order dinner at eight.)
3. Ella ____ las verduras. (preferir — She prefers vegetables.)
4. ¿Qué ____ de desayuno? (querer, tú — What do you want for breakfast?)
5. A mí me ____ las naranjas. (gustar — I like oranges.)
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

You didn't learn a new rule today — you re-used three boot verbs (querer, preferir, pedir) and gustar. Keep them warm: the nosotros form never changes its vowel, and gustar follows the thing liked. Tomorrow we load in the food words that fill these sentences.
