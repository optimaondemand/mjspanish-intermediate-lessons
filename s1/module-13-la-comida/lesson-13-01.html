LESSON 13.01 · SIT DOWN AT THE TABLE
This week is all new words — and no new grammar
Food is everywhere, and so is the Spanish for it. This week you plug new vocabulary into verbs you already own.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You don't need a new grammar rule to talk about food — you just need the words. The verbs are already yours.

Welcome to La comida — food. This is one of the most useful topics in any language, and here's the good news: this week is pure vocabulary. The verbs you'll use to order, ask for, and prefer food — querer, preferir, and pedir — are verbs you already conjugated back in the "boot verb" weeks. So we hang a plate of new words onto an engine you already know how to drive.

🔄 FIRST · VERBS YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. Read these out loud — every one is a verb you already learned as a stem-changer, plus gustar from last year:

Yo quiero… — I want… Yo prefiero… — I prefer… Yo pido… — I order / ask for… Me gusta… — I like…

All three verbs are “boot” (stem-changing) verbs from earlier this year: querer and preferir change e→ie (Module 5), and pedir changes e→i (Module 7). This week you point them all at a menu.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS WEEK YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
name meals, common fruits and vegetables, and restaurant words in Spanish.
2
use verbs you already know — querer, preferir, pedir — to order and say what you want to eat.
3
spot everyday English food words that came from Spanish — some of them older than Spanish itself.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
When you sit down hungry, what do you usually reach for first?
📚 WHY THIS WEEK IS LIGHTER

Every module this year follows one rule: new grammar on known words, or new words on known grammar — never both at once. The last few weeks were heavy on new grammar. This week flips it: the grammar is all review, and your only new job is vocabulary. So breathe easy, and spend your energy making the words stick.

You'll still practice the verbs — but as a warm, familiar tool, not a new puzzle.

★ A WORD ON COURTESY

Sharing a table is one of the oldest ways people show care for one another. Learning to ask politely — “Quiero…”, “¿Me trae la cuenta, por favor?” — is more than grammar; it's courtesy. A guest who can say "please" and "thank you" in someone's own language is always welcome.
