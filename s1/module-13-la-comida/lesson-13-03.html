LESSON 13.03 · NEW WORDS, KNOWN VERBS
The words that fill your plate
Meals, fruits, vegetables, and the restaurant — the whole table in one page.
👋 HOW TO WORK THIS PAGE

These are the food words for the week. Each noun is a naming word, so it carries grammatical gender — watch the color: blue = masculine (el), red = feminine (la). Learning the color with the word saves you from guessing later.

Press play on each word to hear a native voice, say it aloud, then use the flip cards to test yourself. The verbs to use these words are the ones from yesterday — nothing new there.

💡 A GENDER TIP

Most words ending in -a are feminine (la manzana, la cena, la cuenta) and most ending in -o are masculine (el desayuno, el almuerzo). But watch the sneaky ones: el plátano, el tomate, and el menú are all masculine. Learn the color with the word.

📝 PUT THE WORDS TO WORK

Because the verbs are already yours, you can build real sentences right now:

  • Yo quiero una manzana para el desayuno. — I want an apple for breakfast.
  • Nosotros pedimos la cena y miramos el menú. — We order dinner and look at the menu.
  • ¿Prefieres el tomate o la zanahoria? — Do you prefer tomato or carrot?

New words, familiar verbs — exactly the way this week is built.
