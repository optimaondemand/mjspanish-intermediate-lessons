LESSON 9.02 · THE ENGINE
One verb, five forms, one future-making pattern
Learn ir today and you can say where you go and what you're going to do next.
👋 THE ONE IDEA
Ir means "to go." It's irregular — you just memorize its five forms — but once you have them, one small pattern lets you talk about the future.

Most verbs you've met follow a tidy pattern (drop the ending, add the ending). Ir refuses to play along — its forms look nothing like its infinitive. That's fine: it's one of the most common verbs in the language, so you'll learn it fast just by using it. Here is the whole set.

📝 THE FIVE FORMS · ir (to go)
Subject (who)Form of ir
yo (I)voy
tú (you, friendly)vas
usted / él / ella (you-formal / he / she)va
nosotros / nosotras (we)vamos
ustedes / ellos / ellas (you-all / they)van

Five forms to chant: voy / vas / va / vamos / van. Notice we use ustedes ("you all") for a group — the standard form across Latin America, and the one we use all year.

🗺️ USE #1 · WHERE YOU GO: ir + a + place

To say where someone goes, use ir + the little word a ("to") + the place:

Voy + a (to) + la escuela → Voy a la escuela.

One small spelling rule: when a bumps into the masculine el, the two squeeze together into al. It's the only contraction like this in Spanish:

  • a + el parque → Voy al parque. — I go to the park.
  • a + la escuela → Voy a la escuela. — (feminine stays two words: a la.)

You'll meet a whole set of places tomorrow in Lesson 9.03 — today just notice the pattern.

⭐ USE #2 · THE BIG ONE: ir a + infinitive = the near future

Here's the star of the week. To say what someone is going to do, use a form of ir + a + an infinitive (a "to ___" verb you already know):

Voy + a + estudiar → Voy a estudiar.

Keep this in your head: Voy a estudiar = "I'm going to study." This is how Spanish talks about the near future — but the only verb you actually change is ir, and ir is a present-tense verb. You are NOT learning a past tense or a brand-new future tense — just present-tense ir + a + an unchanged infinitive.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Watch both uses work with verbs you already know. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back:

Yo voy a estudiar esta noche. — I'm going to study tonight.
Tú vas a comer con la familia. — You're going to eat with the family.
Nosotros vamos a practicar español. — We're going to practice Spanish.
Ella va al parque. — She goes to the park. (a + el = al)
Ellos van a leer un libro. — They're going to read a book.

Same recipe every time: a form of ir → a → then either a place or an infinitive.

⚡ TRY IT · CONJUGATION DRILL
Pick the correct form of ir. The subject is given, so choose the form that matches. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. Yo ____ a la escuela. (I go to school.)
2. Nosotros ____ a estudiar esta noche. (We're going to study tonight.)
3. Ella ____ a comer ahora. (She's going to eat now.)
4. ¿Tú ____ al parque? (Are you going to the park?)
5. Ustedes ____ a practicar el piano. (You all are going to practice the piano.)
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

Chant the five forms — voy / vas / va / vamos / van — then remember two uses: ir + a + place (where you go, with a + el = al) and ir a + infinitive (what you're going to do). Both are built on the present-tense verb ir.
