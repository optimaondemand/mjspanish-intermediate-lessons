LESSON 9.04 · ALL OF IT TOGETHER
A weekend, in Spanish
See ir a + places and ir a + infinitive working side by side in a real paragraph.
📖 READ & LISTEN · EL FIN DE SEMANA DE SOFÍA

Here is Sofía's weekend. Every sentence uses this week's grammar. Press ▶ on each line to hear it, then read it back:

Los sábados, yo voy al parque con mi familia. — On Saturdays, I go to the park with my family.
Mi hermano va al gimnasio y mi mamá va a la biblioteca. — My brother goes to the gym and my mom goes to the library.
Después, nosotros vamos a comer en un restaurante. — Afterward, we're going to eat at a restaurant.
Por la tarde, yo voy a estudiar para el examen. — In the afternoon, I'm going to study for the test.
¿Adónde vas tú este fin de semana? — Where are you going this weekend?

Did you spot both uses? voy al parque / va a la biblioteca are ir + a + place; vamos a comer / voy a estudiar are the near future (ir a + infinitive). Every verb here is present tense.

🌎 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · PLACE-WORDS HIDING IN ENGLISH

One of this week's words — la plaza — is a word English took straight from Spanish. When you say "the town plaza" in English, you're using a Spanish word without translating it. A plaza is the open public square at the heart of a Spanish-speaking town: people meet there, walk, and gather in the evenings.

English has quietly borrowed a whole set of Spanish "place and space" words. You already say these:

plaza — public square patio — open courtyard cafetería → cafeteria fiesta — party / festival siesta — afternoon rest

A note on parque: it looks like English "park" because both words grew from the same old European root — they're cousins (cognates), which is why it's easy to remember.
