LESSON 1.01 · A NEW YEAR, A RUNNING START
You already know more Spanish than you think
Nothing new to memorize today — just see where you already are, and where this year is headed.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Last year you learned to say who you are. This year you learn to say what you do — and by spring, what you did.

Welcome to M/J Spanish, Intermediate. You come in already able to introduce yourself, describe people, and say what you like. This year we turn all of that into action: you'll learn to talk about what you do every day, what you're doing right now, and what you're going to do next. Today is just a gentle look back at what you own — and a map of where we're going.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. Read these out loud — you already understand every one:

Yo soy… — I am… Estoy bien — I'm doing well Me gusta… — I like… Mi familia es… — My family is…

You already handle SER and ESTAR (the two "to be" verbs), GUSTAR (to like), grammatical gender (el / la), and describing words. This year builds one big new thing on top of that foundation: the rest of the verbs.

📚 A 60-SECOND REFRESHER · SER vs ESTAR

Both mean "to be," but they do different jobs — you already know this in your ear:

  • SER → who/what something is (permanent-ish): Yo soy estudiante. — I am a student.
  • ESTAR → how/where something is (feelings, location): Yo estoy cansado. — I am tired.

Press ▶ to hear each one — reading and hearing a sentence together makes it stick:

Yo soy estudiante. — I am a student.
Yo estoy cansado. — I am tired.

Try the quick sort below — this is review, so trust your instinct.

🗺️ YOUR YEAR AT A GLANCE

Here's the whole road, so you always know where you are:

  • Fall (now): the present tense — what you and others do: regular verbs, then the tricky "boot" verbs, then talking about right now and the near future.
  • Winter: everyday life in Spanish — your routine, food, the city, your body, the weather.
  • Spring (Semester 2): the past tense — how to say what you did and tell a story. That's the big pay-off this whole year builds toward.

★ A WORD ON PERSEVERANCE

A language isn't learned in a sprint — it's learned in small, steady steps. Twenty honest minutes a day beats three rushed hours on a Sunday. That habit of showing up, a little at a time, is perseverance — and it's the real secret to a second year that actually sticks.
