LESSON 1.04 · SPANISH HIDING IN ENGLISH
You already say Spanish every day
The US map is full of Spanish words. Once you know what they mean, you can never un-see it.
🌎 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Spanish isn't a “foreign” language in the United States — it is stitched into the map, the menu, and the way we talk.

Spanish is spoken by roughly 500 million people worldwide, and the United States is one of the largest Spanish-speaking countries on Earth. That long history left fingerprints everywhere — starting with the names of the states and cities you already say out loud. Today is a look, not a test: the graded culture checkpoint comes later in Module 5.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Before today — did you ever think of US place-names as actual Spanish words with meanings?
🗺️ SEE IT · SEVEN NAMES YOU ALREADY SAY

Each of these US place-names is an everyday Spanish word. Read the Spanish, then the meaning — and picture the land the name was describing:

Colorado
colorado
“red / colored” — for the red-rock rivers and canyons.
Nevada
nevada
“snow-covered” — named for its snowy mountains.
Florida
florida
“flowery” — for its flowers and lush green.
Montana
montaña
from montaña, “mountain.”
Amarillo
amarillo
“yellow” — a city in Texas.
Las Vegas
las vegas
“the meadows” — for the grassy springs early travelers found.
El Paso
el paso
“the pass” — a gap through the mountains.

Every one of these is a plain descriptive word. The people who named these places were simply describing what they saw — in Spanish.

💬 WHY THIS MATTERS

Here is the point of noticing this: Spanish is not something happening “somewhere else.” It is the language of the place you live, woven into names you have said your whole life. Learning it isn't picking up a distant, foreign tongue — it is reading the world around you more completely.

All year, each module will highlight one piece of “Spanish in Your World.” Keep a running list of the Spanish you spot in the wild — on maps, menus, and street signs. You will be amazed how long it gets.
