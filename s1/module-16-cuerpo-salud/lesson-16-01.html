LESSON 16.01 · ¡AY, ME DUELE!
Everyone gets sick — this week you learn to say what hurts
Nothing new to memorize today — just wake up what you already know, and see where the week is going.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You already know how to say what you like. This week you use the very same trick to say what hurts.

Welcome to Module 16 — El cuerpo y la salud (the body and health). When you're not feeling well, you need words for the parts of your body and a way to say "my head hurts." Here's the good news: the verb for that, doler (to hurt), behaves exactly like gustar — the "to like" verb you've used since last year. Learn one pattern, get two verbs. Today we just warm up what you own and map the week.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. Read these out loud — you already understand every one:

Me gusta… — I like (one thing) Me gustan… — I like (more than one) Estoy cansado/a — I'm tired Tengo hambre — I'm hungry

Three old friends are about to do all the heavy lifting this week:

  • gustar — notice it changes between me gusta (one thing) and me gustan (more than one). doler does the exact same thing.
  • estar — for how you feel right now: estoy bien, estoy cansado.
  • tener — for the "I have ___" feelings you learned in Module 4: tengo hambre, tengo sueño.

Press ▶ to hear two you already know — reading and hearing together makes it stick:

Me gustan los deportes. — I like sports.
Hoy estoy cansado. — Today I'm tired.

Hold on to me gusta vs. me gustan — tomorrow it becomes me duele vs. me duelen.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS PAGE YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
recall the me gusta / me gustan pattern that this week's new verb copies.
2
tell apart estar (how you feel) and tener (what you "have") — both are health tools you already use.
3
describe what this week covers: a new verb, body words, and a trip to the doctor.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
When you don't feel well, how do you usually let someone know?
🏥 WHY THIS ONE IS WORTH IT

This is some of the most useful Spanish you'll ever learn. Millions of people in the United States speak Spanish, and being able to say me duele aquí ("it hurts here") or understand ¿qué te duele? ("what hurts?") can genuinely help someone — maybe even you — on a hard day.

And you'll notice this week that a lot of medical Spanish is already familiar: hospital, doctor, ambulancia. More on that on Day 4.

★ A WORD ON SERVICE

Language is one of the kindest forms of service. When you can understand someone who is hurting — and help them find the words — you turn a small skill into real care for another person. That is exactly what this week's Spanish is for.
