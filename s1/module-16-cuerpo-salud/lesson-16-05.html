LESSON 16.05 · PULLING IT TOGETHER
You can now say how you feel — and what hurts
A quick review, a self-check, and then the short Module 16 quiz.
📋 THE WEEK IN ONE PLACE
  • doler works like gustar: pronoun (me / te / le / nos / les) + duele (one thing) or duelen (more than one). Me duele la cabeza. / Me duelen los pies.
  • The body: la cabeza, el estómago, la garganta, la mano (feminine!), el brazo, la pierna, el pie, la espalda, la boca, el ojo.
  • Health: estar enfermo/a (to be sick), tener fiebre (to have a fever), tener gripe (to have the flu), el médico / la médica (the doctor).
  • Spanish in your world: medical cognates — hospital, doctor, ambulancia, farmacia — you already understand them.
🔊 SAY IT ONE MORE TIME

Two sentences that use the whole week at once. Press ▶, then say each one aloud:

Me duele la garganta y tengo fiebre. — My throat hurts and I have a fever.
Estoy enfermo, así que voy al médico. — I'm sick, so I'm going to the doctor.
⚡ SELF-CHECK · SAY IT, THEN TAP TO CHECK
Answer each one out loud (or in your head), then tap the card. No score — this is just to see what's solid.
How do you say "My head hurts"?
Me duele la cabeza. (one thing → duele)
How do you say "My feet hurt"?
Me duelen los pies. (more than one → duelen)
Is mano masculine or feminine?
Feminine — la mano, even though it ends in -o.
What does el brazo mean?
the arm
What does tiene fiebre mean?
he/she has a fever
What English word is farmacia a cognate of?
pharmacy (the drugstore — not "farm"!)
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
