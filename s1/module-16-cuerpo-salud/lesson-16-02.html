LESSON 16.02 · ME DUELE / ME DUELEN
If you know gustar, you already know doler
Same pattern, brand-new use. Go slowly — today you learn to say exactly what hurts.
👋 THE ONE IDEA
In Spanish you don't say "I hurt my head." You say "the head hurts to me" — me duele la cabeza. That's the same backwards trick you already use with gustar.

Think about gustar. It doesn't really mean "to like" — it means "to be pleasing." So me gusta el chocolate is literally "chocolate is pleasing to me." The verb doler ("to hurt") works the exact same way: the body part is doing the action, and a little pronoun (me, te, le, nos, les) says who it hurts.

🧩 THE PATTERN YOU ALREADY KNOW
Me gusta el chocolate → Me duele la cabeza

Just like gustar, doler really only shows up in two forms: duele when one thing hurts, and duelen when more than one thing hurts. (Some teachers point out that doler is a "boot" verb like Module 6's poder — the o changes to ue — but with this verb, only duele and duelen ever come up. That's all you need.)

📝 THE WHOLE PATTERN · doler (to hurt)

Pick the pronoun for who hurts, then choose duele (one thing) or duelen (more than one):

Who it hurtsOne thing hurtsMore than one hurts
(a mí) me — meme dueleme duelen
(a ti) te — you (friendly)te duelete duelen
(a usted / él / ella) lele duelele duelen
(a nosotros / nosotras) nos — usnos duelenos duelen
(a ustedes / ellos / ellas) lesles dueleles duelen

The pronouns are me / te / le / nos / les — the same "to-whom" pronouns you met with gustar. Notice we use les for a group ("to you all / to them") — the standard Latin-American form we use all year.

💡 THE ONLY DECISION YOU MAKE

Look at the body part, not the person. One body part → duele. Two or more → duelen. So it's me duele la cabeza (one head) but me duelen los pies (two feet). The verb matches the thing that hurts — exactly like me gusta vs. me gustan.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Watch the pattern work. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back:

Me duele la cabeza. — My head hurts. (one thing → duele)
Me duelen los pies. — My feet hurt. (more than one → duelen)
¿Te duele el estómago? — Does your stomach hurt? (te = to you)
A ella le duele la garganta. — Her throat hurts. (le = to her)
Nos duelen las piernas. — Our legs hurt. (nos = to us; two legs → duelen)

Every time: choose the pronoun for who, then let the body part decide duele or duelen.

🏥 TWO MORE WAYS TO TALK ABOUT HEALTH (you already own both verbs)

Besides doler, two familiar verbs finish the job:

  • estar + feeling — how you are right now: Estoy enfermo. (I'm sick.) · Estoy cansada. (I'm tired.) Remember agreement: a boy says enfermo, a girl enferma.
  • tener + a health noun — what you "have": Tengo fiebre. (I have a fever.) · Tengo gripe. (I have the flu.) · Tengo tos. (I have a cough.)

So you have three tools: doler for what hurts, estar for how you feel, and tener for what you have. You'll load the words themselves tomorrow.

⚡ TRY IT · DUELE OR DUELEN?
Choose the form that matches. Look at the body part: one thing → duele, more than one → duelen. You'll get instant feedback on each one.

"My eyes hurt."  ·  two eyes — duele or duelen?

🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

doler copies gustar: a "to-whom" pronoun (me / te / le / nos / les) plus duele for one thing or duelen for more than one. Say the little chant — me duele, me duelen — and you can already tell anyone what hurts.
