👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Most Spanish verbs are not memorized one at a time. Hundreds of them share one simple pattern — learn it once, use it everywhere.

This week you meet the first big family of Spanish verbs: the ones whose infinitive ends in -AR. Here's the exciting part — a huge number of them are cognates, words so close to English you can already guess them: estudiar (to study), practicar (to practice), usar (to use). Once you learn how the -AR pattern works, you don't just know a few verbs — you can handle any regular -AR verb you ever meet.

🔄 FIRST · SOMETHING YOU ALREADY NOTICED

Back in Module 1 you saw that a verb's ending changes depending on who does the action. You already do this with the "to be" verbs:

Yo soy — I am Tú eres — you are Yo estoy — I am (feeling) Nosotros estamos — we are

Notice: soy / eres and estoy / estamos change their tail depending on the subject. That changing of the ending is called conjugation, and it is the one skill this whole week is built on. The good news: with regular -AR verbs, the endings are predictable — a tidy little set you'll learn tomorrow.

📚 A HEAD START · VERBS YOU ALMOST ALREADY KNOW

Say these out loud. Even before we touch grammar, you can guess what each one means:

  • estudiar → to study
  • practicar → to practice
  • usar → to use
  • preparar → to prepare

See the -ar tail on all of them? That ending is the family name. This week you learn the small set of endings that let you say I study, you study, we study — all from that one word.
