LESSON 2.03 · NEW WORDS, KNOWN PATTERN
Ten -AR verbs to make your own
The pattern is already yours from yesterday — today we just load in the words that ride on it.
👋 WHY THIS IS THE EASY DAY

Yesterday you learned the -AR engine. Today is pure vocabulary — ten common verbs that all follow that same engine. No new grammar to wrestle with: once you know a verb ends in -ar, you already know how to say I ___, you ___, we ___. Listen to each one in a native voice, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

A few are near-cognates — they look like their English meanings. Those are marked cognate so you can lean on words you half-know already.

🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · TEN -AR VERBS

These are infinitives (the "to ___" form). Verbs don't carry gender, so there is no color-coding here — press play and say each one aloud.

  • hablarto speak
  • estudiar cognate · studyto study
  • practicar cognate · practiceto practice
  • escucharto listen (to)
  • usar cognate · useto use
  • caminarto walk
  • mirarto look (at) / to watch
  • trabajarto work
  • cocinarto cook
  • viajarto travel
💡 SPOT THE COGNATES

estudiar → study, practicar → practice, usar → use. When you hear a Spanish verb that sounds like an English one and it means the same thing, that's a cognate — free vocabulary. Tomorrow we'll also meet a few sneaky look-alikes that don't mean what you'd guess.

📝 PUT ONE TO WORK

Because these ride on yesterday's pattern, you can already build sentences with them:

  • Yo escucho música. — I listen to music.
  • Tú caminas a la escuela. — You walk to school.
  • Nosotros viajamos en verano. — We travel in the summer.

Same three steps as always: drop -ar, add the ending for the subject.
