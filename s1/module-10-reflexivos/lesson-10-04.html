LESSON 10.04 · GRAMMAR IN USE
A whole day, in order
See the reflexive verbs and sequence words do their real job: telling a routine as a series of sentences.
👋 READ ANA'S MORNING

Here is one student's rutina diaria (daily routine), told the way you'll soon tell yours — one action after another, in order. Read each line, then press ▶ to hear it. Watch the sequence words primero / luego / después do the work of putting the day in order.

📚 LA RUTINA DIARIA DE ANA
Me llamo Ana, y esta es mi rutina diaria. — My name is Ana, and this is my daily routine.
Primero, me despierto a las seis y media de la mañana. — First, I wake up at six thirty in the morning.
Luego, me levanto y me ducho rápido. — Then, I get up and shower quickly.
Después, me cepillo los dientes y me peino. — After that, I brush my teeth and comb my hair.
Me visto para la escuela, y estoy lista. — I get dressed for school, and I'm ready.
Por la noche, me lavo la cara y me acuesto temprano. — At night, I wash my face and go to bed early.

Notice how Ana never says "my teeth" or "my face" — just los dientes, la cara. The reflexive pronoun already tells you they're hers. That's a very Spanish habit worth copying.

💡 A HANDY MOVE (YOU ALREADY KNOW ir a)

When you talk about your plan for later with ir a + infinitive, the pronoun can hop onto the end of the infinitive: Voy a ducharme. = Me voy a duchar. — both mean "I'm going to shower." Either spot is correct; the safe habit is still pronoun before the conjugated verb.

⚡ TRY IT · DID YOU FOLLOW ANA'S DAY?
Two about her routine, one to complete a line she says. Instant feedback on each.
1. What does Ana do first in the morning?
2. Complete Ana's line: Yo ____ los dientes. (cepillarse)
3. Ana and her brother: Nosotros ____ temprano. (acostarse — We go to bed early.)
🌍 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · A ROUTINE WITH NO ENGLISH WORD

A routine isn't only what you do alone in the morning — it's also how a culture spends its ordinary hours. In much of the Spanish-speaking world there's a daily habit English has no single word for: the sobremesa.

The sobremesa is the time you stay at the table and talk after a meal is finished — plates pushed aside, no rush to leave. It's not the meal and it's not dessert; it's the unhurried conversation that follows, sometimes for an hour or more. The word comes from sobre ("over/upon") + mesa ("table") — literally "over-the-table."

English borrows the word rutina straight into routine — but it has nothing for sobremesa. Learning another language does this a lot: it hands you an idea your own language never bothered to name.
