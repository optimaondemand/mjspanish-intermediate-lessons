LESSON 10.02 · THE PATTERN
One little word, right before the verb
You already conjugate verbs. A reflexive verb adds just one extra word — and it always sits in the same place.
👋 THE ONE IDEA
A reflexive verb is a normal verb plus a reflexive pronoun that means "to/for myself." The pronoun goes right before the conjugated verb.

An infinitive like levantarse carries a little -se on the end — that tag tells you the action loops back on the person doing it (to get oneself up). To use it, you do two things you already know how to do, plus one new one: pick the matching reflexive pronoun, put it before the verb, then conjugate the verb the normal way.

📝 THE FIVE REFLEXIVE PRONOUNS
Subject (who)PronounMeans
yo (I)memyself
tú (you, friendly)teyourself
usted / él / ellaseyourself / himself / herself
nosotros / nosotras (we)nosourselves
ustedes / ellos / ellasseyourselves / themselves

Five pronouns: me / te / se / nos / se. Notice the "you all / they" row uses se and nos for "we" — the Latin-American set we use all year. (There is no separate -áis-style form in this course.)

🧩 PUT IT TOGETHER · levantarse (to get up)

Conjugate levantar exactly like any regular -AR verb, then set the matching pronoun in front:

SubjectPronoun + verbMeaning
yome levantoI get (myself) up
túte levantasyou get up
usted / él / ellase levantayou / he / she gets up
nosotros / nosotrasnos levantamoswe get up
ustedes / ellos / ellasse levantanyou all / they get up

The verb endings are the same -o / -as / -a / -amos / -an you already know. The only new move is the pronoun in front — and it must match the subject.

📍 THE PLACEMENT RULE
me (pronoun) + levanto (verb) = Yo me levanto

The reflexive pronoun goes right before the conjugated verb: Yo me levanto. Not “Yo levanto me.” Say the pronoun first, then the verb — every time.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Same routine verbs, every subject. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back — listen for the little pronoun in front:

Yo me levanto a las siete. — I get up at seven.
Tú te lavas las manos. — You wash your hands.
Ella se ducha por la mañana. — She showers in the morning.
Nosotros nos cepillamos los dientes. — We brush our teeth.
Ellos se peinan. — They comb their hair.

Small thing worth noticing: with body parts, Spanish says las manos / los dientes ("the hands / the teeth"), not "my." The reflexive pronoun already tells you whose they are.

⚡ TRY IT · CONJUGATION DRILL
Pick the form that matches the subject — the right pronoun and the right ending. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. Yo ____ a las seis. (levantarse — I get up at six.)
2. María ____ la cara. (lavarse — María washes her face.)
3. Nosotros ____ temprano. (levantarse — We get up early.)
4. ¿Tú ____ por la mañana? (ducharse — Do you shower in the morning?)
5. Los niños ____ los dientes. (cepillarse — The children brush their teeth.)
⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · INFINITIVE → "YO" FORM
Predict the "yo" (I) form, then tap the card to check.
lavarse — to wash oneself  ·  What is the yo form?
me lavo — I wash myself.  Pronoun me in front → verb lavo.
💡 ONE HEADS-UP FOR TOMORROW

A few routine verbs are also "boot" stem-changers you already know — despertarse (e→ie: me despierto), acostarse (o→ue: me acuesto), vestirse (e→i: me visto). The pronoun rule doesn't change one bit — you just add the stem change you already do. We'll meet them all in Lesson 10.03.

🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

A reflexive verb = reflexive pronoun (me / te / se / nos / se) + the verb conjugated as usual, with the pronoun right before the verb. Match the pronoun to the subject and you're done: Yo me levanto, tú te levantas, ella se levanta.
