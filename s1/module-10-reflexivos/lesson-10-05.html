📚 THE WEEK IN THREE MOVES
  • The pronoun rule. A reflexive verb takes a pronoun me / te / se / nos / se, placed right before the conjugated verb: Yo me levanto.
  • Eight routine verbs. despertarse, levantarse, ducharse, lavarse, cepillarse, peinarse, vestirse, acostarse — three are also "boot" verbs (me despierto, me visto, me acuesto).
  • Sequence words. primero / luego / después string single actions into a series — a real routine.
🧩 ONE VERB, ALL FIVE FORMS · levantarse
SubjectPronoun + verbMeaning
yome levantoI get up
túte levantasyou get up
usted / él / ellase levantayou / he / she gets up
nosotros / nosotrasnos levantamoswe get up
ustedes / ellos / ellasse levantanyou all / they get up

Boot reminder: stem-changers change in every form except nosotros. So it's me acuesto but nos acostamos; me despierto but nos despertamos.

❓ QUICK SELF-CHECK · ANSWER, THEN OPEN
Where does the reflexive pronoun go?
Right before the conjugated verb: Yo me ducho. Never after it. (With ir a + infinitive it may also attach to the end of the infinitive: Voy a ducharme.)
What is the "yo" form of despertarse (e→ie)?
me despierto — pronoun me in front, and the boot change e→ie in the stem.
What is the "nosotros" form of acostarse (o→ue)?
nos acostamos — pronoun nos, and no stem change (boot verbs never change in the nosotros form).
⚡ MIXED REVIEW · PICK THE RIGHT FORM
Four sentences, all subjects, some boot verbs. Choose the form that matches — instant feedback on each.
1. Yo ____ a las seis y media. (despertarse — I wake up at six thirty.)
2. Nosotros ____ tarde los viernes. (acostarse — We go to bed late on Fridays.)
3. María ____ rápido. (vestirse — María gets dressed quickly.)
4. ¿Tú ____ antes de la escuela? (peinarse — Do you comb your hair before school?)
