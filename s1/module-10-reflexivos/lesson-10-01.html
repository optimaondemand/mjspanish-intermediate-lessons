👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You already say what you do. This week you learn to say what you do to yourself — wake up, get up, shower, get dressed.

Welcome to Module 10. So far you can say what you do (yo estudio), what you're doing right now (estoy estudiando), and what you're going to do (voy a estudiar). This week adds the last everyday piece: your daily routine — the things you do to yourself each morning and night. Spanish marks these with one small extra word, and you'll have it down by Friday.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. Read these out loud — you built every one over the last nine weeks:

Yo estudio… — I study… Estoy comiendo — I'm eating (right now) Voy a viajar — I'm going to travel Primero… luego… — First… then…

You already handle regular verbs, the "boot" stem-changers, the present progressive, and ir a + infinitive for the near future. This week hangs one new idea on top of verbs you can already conjugate.

📚 A 60-SECOND PREVIEW · TO SOMETHING vs TO YOURSELF

Here is the whole idea in one pair of sentences. The verb is the same — but the little word me changes who receives the action:

Yo lavo el carro. — I wash the car. (I do it to something else)
Yo me lavo. — I wash myself. (I do it to me)

When the action loops back on the person doing it — you wake yourself, dress yourself — Spanish adds a reflexive pronoun like me. That's the whole week in a nutshell. Tomorrow we make it exact.

🕐 YOUR ROUTINE, COMING THIS WEEK

By Friday you'll say all of this about yourself, in order:

  • Morning: me despierto (I wake up) → me levanto (I get up) → me ducho (I shower) → me visto (I get dressed).
  • Getting ready: me cepillo los dientes (I brush my teeth) → me peino (I comb my hair).
  • Night: me lavo la cara (I wash my face) → me acuesto (I go to bed).

★ A WORD ON SELF-GOVERNMENT

A routine is self-government in its smallest, truest form: the quiet decisions you make each morning before anyone is watching. Learning to name your routine in another language is a good moment to notice it — and maybe to shape it a little on purpose. Self-government starts with the ordinary hour you rarely think about.
