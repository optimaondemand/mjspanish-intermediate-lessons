🔑 THE WHOLE WEEK ON ONE CARD

Three formulas, four special words, one superlative move. The describing word always agrees with what it describes.

What you meanPatternExample
more … thanmás + adj + quemás alta que
less … thanmenos + adj + quemenos alto que
as … astan + adj + comotan grande como
better / worsemejor / peor + quemejor que
older / younger (age)mayor / menor + quemenor que
the most / least of allel/la más + adj + dela más alta de la clase

The one thing to guard: más and menos take que, but tan takes como — and a superlative ends in de, never en.

🌐 AND THE FALSE FRIENDS

Six Spanish words that look like English but don't match: embarazada (pregnant, not embarrassed), éxito (success, not exit), ropa (clothes, not rope), librería (bookstore, not library), sopa (soup, not soap), asistir (to attend, not assist).

⚡ MIXED SELF-CHECK · PATTERNS + SPECIAL WORDS + FALSE FRIENDS
A little of everything from this week. Pick one per row for instant feedback — then you're ready for the quiz.
1. El elefante es ____ el ratón. (bigger than)
2. Mi carro es ____ tu carro. (as fast as)
3. Mi hermana tiene 8 años; yo tengo 12. Mi hermana es ____ yo. (younger than me)
4. Este libro es ____ de la biblioteca. (the best in the library)
5. What does the Spanish word ropa mean?

★ A WORD ON COURTESY

This week you gained a powerful tool: the ability to say what is more, less, and best. Use it to notice good things — a kinder friend, a better book, a stronger effort — more than to rank people against each other. Comparing well is partly a grammar skill and partly a habit of courtesy. Carry both forward.
