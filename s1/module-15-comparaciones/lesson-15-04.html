📖 READ · MI FAMILIA

Every sentence below uses one of this week's comparisons, in the present tense. Read it once for the gist, then press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back.

Mi hermano Diego es más alto que yo. — My brother Diego is taller than me.
Pero yo soy menor que Diego; él tiene quince años. — But I am younger than Diego; he is fifteen.
Nuestro perro es tan grande como nuestro gato. — Our dog is as big as our cat.
Para mí, la sopa de mamá es mejor que la pizza. — For me, Mom's soup is better than pizza.
Mi mamá es la más simpática de toda la familia. — My mom is the nicest in the whole family.

🌐 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · FALSE FRIENDS

You noticed sopa in the reading. It looks like English “soap” — but it means soup. Words like this are called false friends (in Spanish, falsos amigos): they look or sound like an English word but mean something completely different. Most Spanish–English look-alikes are real cognates that do match (importante = important). False friends are the sneaky exceptions — and knowing them saves you from some very funny mistakes.

A tip for two tricky ones: a librería is where you buy books (a bookstore); the place you borrow them is a biblioteca. And when you asistir a a class, you attend it — you don't “assist” the teacher.

💡 WHY THIS MATTERS

Cognates give you free vocabulary — but a handful of false friends can trip you up. These six are on the Module 15 check, so make sure you can catch them: embarazada (pregnant), éxito (success), ropa (clothes), librería (bookstore), sopa (soup), asistir (to attend).
