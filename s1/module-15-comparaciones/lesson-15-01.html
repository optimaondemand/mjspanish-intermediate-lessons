LESSON 15.01 · TALLER, SHORTER, BETTER, WORSE
This week you learn to compare
Nothing new to memorize today — just wake up the describing words you already own, and see where the week is headed.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You can already say what someone is like. This week you learn to say who is more, less, or just as much — and who is the most of all.

Welcome to Module 15. All year you've described people and things one at a time: Ana es alta. — Ana is tall. This week you set two of them side by side and compare: Ana es más alta que Luis. — Ana is taller than Luis. Same describing words you already know — you just add a few small comparing words around them. Today is only a warm-up and a road map.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. Read these out loud — you already understand every one:

Ana es alta. — Ana is tall. El libro es bueno. — The book is good. El perro es grande. — The dog is big. Yo soy joven. — I am young.

You already handle the verb SER (to be) and a whole set of describing words — alto (tall), bajo (short), grande (big), bueno (good), joven (young). And you know adjectives agree: alto for a boy, alta for a girl. This week builds one new move on top of all that: comparing.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS WEEK YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
compare two things as more, less, or as…as — más…que, menos…que, tan…como.
2
use the four special comparing words — mejor (better), peor (worse), mayor (older), menor (younger).
3
say which one is the most of all — Ana es la más alta de la clase.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Comparing is something you do all day in English — “this one's better,” “she's taller.” How does it feel to do it in Spanish?
📚 A 60-SECOND PEEK · THE SHAPE OF A COMPARISON

You don't have to build this yet — just notice the shape. A comparison in Spanish wraps a small word before your describing word and a small word after it:

  • más alto que → more tall than = taller than
  • menos alto que → less tall than = less tall than
  • tan alto como → as tall as = as tall as

Press ▶ to hear a real comparison — reading and hearing it together makes the shape stick:

Ana es más alta que Luis. — Ana is taller than Luis.
Luis es tan simpático como Ana. — Luis is as nice as Ana.

See it? The describing word in the middle is one you already know. Tomorrow you learn to build these yourself.

★ A WORD ON COURTESY

Comparing is powerful — and it asks for care. Saying one thing is better than another is fine for books, teams, and weather; using it to put a person down is not. As you learn these words, practice using them kindly and honestly. That habit of measuring your words is courtesy — and it makes your Spanish worth listening to.
