LESSON 15.02 · THE PATTERN
Más … que, and its whole family
One tidy formula does most of the work. Learn it once and it fits every describing word you know.
👋 THE ONE IDEA
Wrap your describing word: put a “how much” word in front and a “compared to” word behind.

To compare two things in Spanish, you keep your describing word in the middle (alto, grande, simpático…) and add two small words around it. There are three flavors: more, less, and as…as. Remember: the describing word still agrees with what it describes — más alta for a girl, más alto for a boy.

🧩 THE THREE FORMULAS
más + describing word + que = more … than
menos + describing word + que = less … than
tan + describing word + como = as … as

Watch the pairs: más and menos both take que (“than”). But tan takes como (“as”) — never que. That pairing is the one thing to guard.

📝 SEE THE THREE IN ACTION
FormulaSpanishEnglish
más … queAna es más alta que Luis.Ana is taller than Luis.
menos … queLuis es menos alto que Ana.Luis is less tall than Ana.
tan … comoAna es tan simpática como Luis.Ana is as nice as Luis.

Press ▶ to hear each one and say it back:

Ana es más alta que Luis. — Ana is taller than Luis.
Ana es tan simpática como Luis. — Ana is as nice as Luis.
⚠️ FOUR SPECIAL WORDS · NO “MÁS” NEEDED

Four common comparisons have their own word — you do not add más in front of them. English does the same thing: we say better, not “more good.”

Instead of…UseMeaningExample
más buenomejorbetterEste libro es mejor que ese libro.
más malopeorworseHoy el clima es peor que ayer.
más viejo (age)mayorolderMi hermano es mayor que yo.
más joven (age)menoryoungerMi hermana es menor que yo.

They still use que for “than.” Just never put más in front — the word already carries the “more.” Use mayor/menor for a person's age.

🏆 THE MOST OF ALL · SUPERLATIVES

When something is the most or the least of a whole group, add the (el / la / los / las) and finish with de for “in” or “of”:

el / la + más / menos + describing word + de
SpanishEnglish
Ana es la más alta de la clase.Ana is the tallest in the class.
Luis es el menos alto de la clase.Luis is the least tall in the class.
Este libro es el mejor de la biblioteca.This book is the best in the library.

Two things to notice: the el / la and the describing word both agree with what you describe, and the “in / of” word is de — not en. The irregular mejor/peor work here too: el mejor = the best.

⚡ TRY IT · BUILD THE COMPARISON
Pick the phrase that correctly finishes each sentence. Watch the pairs (más/menos…que, tan…como) and the agreement. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. Ana mide 1.70 m; Luis mide 1.60 m. Ana es ____ Luis. (Ana is taller than Luis.)
2. Un elefante y un ratón: el elefante es ____ el ratón. (bigger than)
3. Ana y Luis son igual de amables. Ana es ____ Luis. (as nice as)
4. Para mí, este libro es ____ ese libro. (this book is better than that one)
5. Mi hermano tiene 15 años; yo tengo 12. Mi hermano es ____ yo. (older than me)
6. Ana es la chica más alta ____ la clase. (the tallest girl in the class)
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

One formula, three flavors: más…que (more), menos…que (less), tan…como (as…as). Four words go their own way: mejor, peor, mayor, menor (no más). And for the most of all, add el / la and finish with de. The describing word always agrees.
