LESSON 15.03 · THE WORDS YOU COMPARE WITH
Small words, big difference
The pattern is already yours from yesterday — today we lock in the little comparing words and the describing words that ride on them.
👋 TWO KINDS OF WORDS TODAY

Comparing takes two kinds of words. First, the tiny comparing words that build the frame — más, menos, que, tan, como, and the four special ones. Second, the describing words you drop into the middle — and those are old friends from earlier this year. Listen to each in a native voice, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · THE COMPARING WORDS

These little words carry no gender, so there's no color-coding here — press play and say each one aloud.

  • másmore
  • menosless / fewer
  • quethan
  • tanas / so
  • comoas / like
  • mejorbetter
  • peorworse
  • mayorolder (in age)
  • menoryounger (in age)
🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · DESCRIBING WORDS YOU ALREADY KNOW

These are all from earlier this year — the words you drop into the middle of a comparison. They agree in gender and number with what they describe (alto / alta), so their citation form is shown here without color.

  • alto / altatall
  • bajo / bajashort (in height)
  • grandebig / large
  • pequeño / pequeñasmall / little
  • rápido / rápidafast
  • fuertestrong

💡 A QUICK REMINDER ON GENDER

The nouns keep their color code: el perro, el libro are masculine (blue); la casa is feminine (red). Your describing word agrees with the noun — La casa es pequeña, El perro es pequeño — and that agreement carries straight into your comparisons.

📝 PUT THEM TO WORK

Because these ride on yesterday's pattern, you can already build real comparisons with them:

  • Mi casa es más grande que tu casa. — My house is bigger than your house.
  • El perro es tan rápido como el gato. — The dog is as fast as the cat.
  • Este libro es mejor que ese libro. — This book is better than that book.

Same frame every time: comparing word → describing word → comparing word.
