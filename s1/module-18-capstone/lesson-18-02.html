LESSON 18.02 · ONE PAGE, EVERY VERB TYPE
The whole present-tense verb system
No new rules today — a clean map of everything you learned, then a mixed drill to prove it sticks.
👋 THE BIG PICTURE
Every present-tense verb you know fits into one of three groups. Sort the verb first, then conjugate.

This semester you built the present tense in layers: regular verbs, then a few yo-go irregulars, then the boot (stem-changing) verbs. Here they are, side by side, so you can see the whole system at once. Read each table, then take the mixed drill.

📝 1 · REGULAR VERBS — drop the ending, add the ending
Subject (who)-AR (hablar)-ER (comer)-IR (vivir)
yohablocomovivo
túhablascomesvives
usted / él / ellahablacomevive
nosotros / nosotrashablamoscomemosvivimos
ustedes / ellos / ellashablancomenviven

Notice: -ER and -IR are almost identical — they only differ in the nosotros row (-emos vs -imos). And we always use ustedes for a group — the Latin-American form we've used all year.

📝 2 · YO-GO VERBS — only the "yo" form is special

These verbs are regular in every form except yo, where they add a surprise -go:

tener → tengo hacer → hago poner → pongo salir → salgo venir → vengo decir → digo traer → traigo

So yo tengo sueño, but tú tienes, nosotros tenemos. Learn the yo form; the rest follow the normal endings (with tener and venir also boot-changing e→ie in the other forms).

📝 3 · BOOT VERBS — the stem changes in the "boot," not in nosotros

In four of the five forms the stem vowel changes. The one form that keeps the original stem is nosotros — that's the notch outside the "boot."

e → ie: querer → quiero · o → ue: poder → puedo · e → i: pedir → pido

Watch the trap: nosotros does not change — queremos, podemos, pedimos (never "quieremos"). Everything else takes the change: quiero, quieres, quiere, quieren.

⚡ MIXED DRILL · ALL THREE VERB TYPES
One from each family, shuffled. Sort the verb type first, then pick the form that matches the subject. Instant feedback on each row.
1. Yo ____ español todos los días. (estudiar — regular -AR)
2. Nosotros ____ en la cafetería. (comer — regular -ER)
3. Yo ____ la tarea después de clase. (hacer — yo-go)
4. Ella ____ jugar al fútbol. (querer — boot e→ie)
5. Ellos ____ dormir ocho horas. (poder — boot o→ue)
6. Nosotros ____ ocho horas cada noche. (dormir — boot o→ue, nosotros trap)
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

Three groups, one habit: sort the verb, then conjugate. Is it regular (just endings), yo-go (only the yo is special), or a boot verb (stem changes everywhere but nosotros)? Answer that first and the form falls right out.
