LESSON 18.05 · PUT IT ALL TOGETHER
Build your Un día en mi vida
One page to build the recording, check yourself, and finish Semester 1 strong.
🛠️ BUILD IT AS A SERIES OF SENTENCES
Don't try to say everything at once. Build one short sentence at a time, in the order your day happens.

Aim for six to eight short sentences that add up to about 60–90 seconds. Follow this shape (present tense throughout), then swap in your own details:

  • Open: Me llamo ______, y esta es mi rutina.
  • Morning: Por la mañana, primero me levanto y me ducho.
  • Midday: Luego estudio en casa y como con mi familia.
  • Afternoon: Por la tarde, juego al fútbol / practico el piano / hago la tarea.
  • Evening: Por la noche, me lavo la cara y me acuesto.
  • Close (near future): Mañana voy a ______.

Use the sequence words primero, luego, después to glue the sentences into a day — that's what turns a list into a story.

⚡ SELF-CHECK · IS EACH SENTENCE READY?
Pick the form that makes each capstone sentence correct. Present tense (and near future) only — instant feedback on each.
1. Por la mañana, yo ____ a las siete. (levantarse)
2. Por la tarde, yo ____ al fútbol. (jugar)
3. Esta noche, yo ____ un libro. ("I'm going to read a book.")
✅ READINESS CHECKLIST · BEFORE YOU SUBMIT

Read your notes out loud once, then check each box in your head:

  • ✓ My sentences are in order (morning → midday → afternoon → evening).
  • ✓ I use at least six sentences — a series, not single words.
  • ✓ Everything is in the present tense (plus one voy a + infinitive for the near future).
  • ✓ I include some reflexive routine verbs (me levanto, me ducho, me acuesto…).
  • ✓ I use the sequence words primero / luego / después.
  • ✓ My clip is about 60–90 seconds, and I can be heard clearly.
🎤 NOW: RECORD YOUR CAPSTONE
Record Un día en mi vida in Canvas

Open the capstone assignment and use the media recorder to record your day as a series of sentences (about 60–90 seconds). You may read from your notes, and you can re-record as many times as you like before you submit. This is graded on a friendly 4-point rubric — comprehensibility, accuracy, range, and delivery. Speak clearly and be proud of it.

📋 AND: THE SEMESTER 1 FINAL CHECK
Take the Semester 1 Final Check

One short cumulative check (about 20–25 questions) that samples across the whole semester — every verb type, near future, reflexives, object pronouns, saber / conocer, comparatives, core vocabulary, and a few culture-link items. It's worth 5% of your grade and is present tense only — nothing you haven't already practiced. Review Lessons 18.02–18.03 first, then take it with confidence.

📝 ALSO THIS WEEK · THE MODULE 18 CHECK

There's also a short Module 18 Check (about 10 questions, unlimited attempts, 70% to complete the module) — a light review of this week's big ideas. It's separate from the Semester 1 Final Check above. Take it whenever you're ready.

★ A WORD ON SELF-GOVERNMENT

A whole semester of showing up — twenty minutes at a time, one small step after another — is what put a full day of Spanish in your own mouth. Recording your own voice takes a little courage, too. Do your honest best, submit your real work, and take pride in how far steady effort carried you. That's self-government: choosing the good habit even when no one's watching.

➡️ WHAT'S NEXT · ¡NOS VEMOS EN EL SEMESTRE 2!
You can say what you do. Next: what you did.

All year you've lived in the present tense — today, right now, and the near future. In Semester 2 you'll unlock the biggest new power in the language: the past tense. You'll learn to tell what happened — what you did yesterday, stories, and memories. That's how you go from describing a routine to telling a real tale.

You won't need any past-tense Spanish yet — that's exactly what we build next semester, step by step, the same gentle way you built the present tense this fall. For now: finish your capstone, take the final check, and rest easy. ¡Buen trabajo, y nos vemos en el Semestre 2!
