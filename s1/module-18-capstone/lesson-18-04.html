🌐 COGNATES — YOUR BRIDGE INTO SPANISH
All year, look-alike words gave you a running start — and Spanish is hiding in English more than you noticed.

Words that look alike and mean the same thing are cognates (estudiar → study, practicar → practice, comprender → comprehend). And English borrowed loads of everyday words straight from Spanish — rodeo, patio, plaza, cañón (canyon), mosquito, fiesta, siesta. A few words even traveled through Spanish from Indigenous languages, like tomate (tomato) and chocolate from Nahuatl.

One caution you learned: watch the false friends. Librería is a bookstore, not a library; éxito means success, not exit; ropa means clothes, not rope.

📖 A SHORT READING · PRESS ▶ TO HEAR EACH LINE

Read each Spanish line, then press ▶ to hear it. Reading and hearing together makes it stick:

El español está en todas partes en los Estados Unidos. — Spanish is everywhere in the United States.
Muchas palabras del inglés vienen del español, como rodeo, patio y plaza. — Many English words come from Spanish, like rodeo, patio, and plaza.
Cuando yo leo una palabra cognada, comprendo más rápido. — When I read a cognate word, I understand faster.

Notice: every verb there is one you know — está (estar), vienen (venir, yo-go), leo and comprendo (regular). Present tense, start to finish.

⚡ CULTURE CHECK · SPANISH HIDING IN ENGLISH
Pick the best answer for each. These are the kinds of links you'll see on the Module 18 culture-link items.
1. The English word canyon comes from which Spanish word?
2. In Spanish, librería actually means…
3. comprender is a cognate of which English verb?
4. The word tomate (English tomato) came into Spanish from which Indigenous language?
🎤 MODEL · DIEGO'S "UN DÍA EN MI VIDA"

Here's exactly the shape your capstone should take — a short series of sentences, in order, in the present tense, ending with one near-future plan. Press ▶ to hear each line:

Me llamo Diego, y esta es mi rutina. — My name is Diego, and this is my routine.
Por la mañana, primero me levanto y me ducho. — In the morning, first I get up and shower. (reflexives + primero)
Luego estudio en casa y como con mi familia. — Then I study at home and eat with my family. (regular -ar / -er)
Por la tarde, juego al fútbol y hago la tarea. — In the afternoon, I play soccer and do my homework. (boot jugar + yo-go hacer)
Esta noche voy a leer un libro. — Tonight I'm going to read a book. (near future: ir a + infinitive)

Five short sentences — morning, midday, afternoon, and a near-future closer. Copy the shape, change the details to your own life. That's your capstone.
