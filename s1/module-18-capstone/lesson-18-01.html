👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You started the year saying who you are. You're ending it saying what you do, all day long — in your own voice.

Welcome to the last module of Semester 1. This week has no new grammar. Instead, you'll gather the whole semester into one thing you can actually say and record: a day in your life, told as a series of sentences. Today you'll preview that capstone and take a quick look back at everything you now own.

🎤 YOUR CAPSTONE · UN DÍA EN MI VIDA

By Friday you'll record a short clip — about 60 to 90 seconds — narrating a typical day in Spanish: what you do in the morning, the afternoon, and the evening, plus one thing you're going to do next.

You'll speak in a series of sentences (not single words), using the present tense, your daily-routine (reflexive) verbs, and ir a + infinitive for the near future. You may write notes and read from them — and we build up to it one day at a time. This is your victory lap.

🔄 LOOK HOW FAR YOU CAME

You are not the same Spanish student you were in August. Read these out loud — you built every one this semester:

Present tense — -ar / -er / -ir Yo-go — tengo, hago, salgo Boot verbs — quiero, puedo, pido Right now — estoy hablando Near future — voy a viajar My routine — me levanto lo / la — object pronouns saber / conocer

Every one of those was brand-new this semester. Now they're tools you own — and this week you put them all in one place.

🗺️ THE SEMESTER AT A GLANCE

Here's the whole road you traveled — and where it lands this week:

  • Verbs (Modules 2–7): the present tense — regular verbs, then the "boot" (stem-changing) verbs.
  • Talking about life (Modules 8–12): what you're doing right now, your plans, your daily routine, object pronouns, and saber vs. conocer.
  • Your world (Modules 13–17): food, the city, comparisons, the body & health, and the weather.
  • This week (Module 18): pull it all together and record Un día en mi vida.

★ A WORD ON PERSEVERANCE

Eighteen weeks ago, a single Spanish sentence took real effort. This week you'll string a whole day's worth together. That didn't happen in a burst — it happened twenty minutes at a time, week after week. Finishing what you start, one small step at a time, is perseverance — and it's the reason you can do this capstone at all.
