📖 READ · UN VIAJE POR LOS ESTADOS UNIDOS

Every bold verb below is one of your new e→ie boot verbs, in the present tense. Read it once for the gist, then press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back.

Yo quiero viajar por los Estados Unidos. — I want to travel through the United States.
Prefiero los lugares con nombres en español. — I prefer places with names in Spanish.
El viaje empieza en El Paso, Texas. — The trip begins in El Paso, Texas.
Mi hermana piensa visitar Colorado y Nevada. — My sister plans to visit Colorado and Nevada.
¿Entiendes por qué me gustan estos nombres? — Do you understand why I like these names?
WORD HELP
el viajethe triplos lugaresthe places

Notice the boot change in quiero, prefiero, empieza, piensa, and entiendes — every one has the ie, because none of them is a nosotros form.

🌐 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · US PLACE-NAMES

Here's something remarkable: you have been speaking a little Spanish your whole life without knowing it. Centuries ago, Spanish explorers and settlers traveled across what is now the western and southern United States, and they named the land in Spanish. Those names stuck. Today they are the everyday names of US states and cities — and each one still means something in Spanish.

Once you know this, the map turns into a Spanish vocabulary list. Every time you say Colorado or Florida, you're describing the land the way its Spanish namers saw it.

💡 WHY THIS MATTERS

Spanish isn't a faraway language — it's woven right into the United States, in the names of the places you already live near and travel to. These place-names are on the Module 5 check, so make sure you can match each one to its meaning: Colorado (red), Nevada (snow-covered), Florida (flowery), Montana (mountain), Amarillo (yellow), Las Vegas (the meadows), El Paso (the pass).
