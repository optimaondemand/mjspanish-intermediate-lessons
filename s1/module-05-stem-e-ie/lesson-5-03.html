LESSON 5.03 · NEW WORDS, KNOWN PATTERN
Eight boot verbs to make your own
You already own the e→ie pattern — today we just load in the words that ride on it.
👋 WHY THIS IS THE EASY DAY

Yesterday you learned the e→ie engine with querer and pensar. Today is pure vocabulary — eight common verbs that all follow that same boot pattern. Once you know a verb is a boot verb, you already know to change e to ie everywhere except nosotros. Listen to each one in a native voice, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

A couple are near-cognates — they hint at their English meanings. Those are marked cognate so you can lean on words you half-know already.

🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · EIGHT BOOT VERBS (e→ie)

These are infinitives (the "to ___" form). Verbs don't carry gender, so there is no color-coding here — press play and say each one aloud.

  • quererto want (also: to love)
  • pensarto think
  • empezarto begin, to start
  • comenzar cognate · commenceto begin, to start
  • entenderto understand
  • preferir cognate · preferto prefer
  • cerrarto close
  • perderto lose; to miss (a bus, an event)
💡 SPOT THE COGNATES

preferir → prefer, comenzar → commence (a fancy word for "begin"). When a Spanish verb echoes an English one and means the same thing, that's a cognate — free vocabulary. Note that empezar and comenzar are twins: both mean "to begin," and both are boot verbs.

📝 PUT ONE TO WORK

Because these ride on yesterday's pattern, you can already build sentences with them — using verbs and words you already know:

  • Yo quiero estudiar español. — I want to study Spanish.
  • Tú prefieres escuchar música. — You prefer to listen to music.
  • La clase empieza a las ocho. — The class begins at eight.
  • Nosotros entendemos la lección. — We understand the lesson. (nosotros = no change!)

Same rule every time: change e to ie — except in nosotros.
