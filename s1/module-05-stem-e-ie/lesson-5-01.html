LESSON 5.01 · A FAMILIAR VERB WITH A SECRET
You already met a "boot" verb — you just didn't know its name
Nothing brand-new to fear today — just a small vowel twist you have already been saying.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Most verbs keep their stem exactly the same. A special family changes one vowel in the middle — e becomes ie — in every form except nosotros.

By now you can conjugate regular -AR, -ER, and -IR verbs, and you've handled the irregular yo-go verbs like tener. This week we meet a new pattern that sits right on top of everything you already know: stem-changing verbs. The endings do not change at all — only one vowel inside the verb shifts. Today we just notice the pattern; the rest of the week we make it automatic.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting cold. Read these out loud — every one is already yours from earlier modules:

yo hablo — I speak tú comes — you eat nosotros vivimos — we live yo tengo — I have

Now look closely at tener (to have), which you learned back in Module 4. You already say tú tienes, ella tiene, ellos tienen — but nosotros tenemos. Notice it? The e in ten- turns into ie everywhere except the nosotros form.

Tener was your very first “boot” verb — you've been saying the pattern for weeks. This week we give it a name and add a whole family of verbs that do the same thing.

👤 WHY "BOOT"?

Some teachers call these “boot” verbs (you may also hear shoe verbs). Here's why: if you write out the five forms in a column and draw a line around the ones that change, the line makes the shape of a boot. The nosotros form is the only one left outside the boot — and it's the only one that does not change.

  • yo, tú, usted/él/ella, and ustedes/ellos/ellas → inside the boot → e becomes ie
  • nosotros / nosotras → outside the boot → stem stays the same

Tomorrow you'll see the full table and drill it. The endings never change — they're the same -o / -es / -e / -emos / -en and -o / -as / -a / -amos / -an you already know.
