LESSON 7.03 · NEW WORDS, KNOWN PATTERN
Four e→i verbs to make your own
The pattern is already yours from yesterday — today we just load in the words that ride on it.
👋 WHY THIS IS THE EASY DAY

Yesterday you learned the e→i engine. Today is pure vocabulary — four common verbs that all follow that same engine. No new grammar to wrestle with: once you know a verb is an e→i boot verb, you already know that the stem shows an i everywhere except nosotros. Listen to each one in a native voice, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

Two of these are near-cognates — they sound like their English meanings, so lean on them. Those are marked cognate.

🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · FOUR E→I VERBS

These are infinitives (the “to ___” form). Verbs don't carry gender, so there is no color-coding here — press play and say each one aloud.

  • pedirto ask for / to order (food)
  • servir cognate · serveto serve
  • repetir cognate · repeatto repeat
  • seguirto follow / to continue
💡 SPOT THE COGNATES

repetir → repeat, servir → serve. When a Spanish verb sounds like an English one and means the same thing, that's a cognate — nearly free vocabulary. And pedir is worth knowing well: at a restaurant it means “to order.”

📝 PUT ONE TO WORK

Because these ride on yesterday's pattern, you can already build sentences with them:

  • Yo pido un jugo. — I ask for a juice.
  • El camarero sirve la sopa. — The waiter serves the soup.
  • Nosotros seguimos el mapa. — We follow the map. (nosotros = no change!)

Same rule as always: the stem shows an i for every subject except nosotros.
