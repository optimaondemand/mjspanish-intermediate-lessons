LESSON 7.01 · THE LAST BOOT
You already know how “boot” verbs work — this week just adds the third kind
Nothing brand-new to wrestle with today — just wake up what you already own and see where the week is headed.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You have met two boot families already. This week you meet the third and last one — and the rule is exactly the same shape you already know.

In Module 5 you learned e→ie boot verbs (like querer → quiero), and in Module 6 you learned o→ue and jugar's u→ue (like poder → puedo, jugar → juego). This week completes the set with e→i verbs. The good news: every boot verb follows the same shape, so you are not really learning something new — you are finishing a pattern you already half-own.

🔄 FIRST · WAKE UP YOUR BOOT VERBS

You are not starting from zero. Read these out loud — every one is a boot verb you already met:

quiero — I want (e→ie) puedo — I can (o→ue) juego — I play (u→ue) duermo — I sleep (o→ue)

Remember the one thing that makes them “boot” verbs: the stem changes in every present form except nosotros. Press ▶ to hear it in a real sentence:

Yo quiero agua. — I want water. (stem changes: quer- → quier-)
Nosotros jugamos al fútbol. — We play soccer. (nosotros = no change!)
