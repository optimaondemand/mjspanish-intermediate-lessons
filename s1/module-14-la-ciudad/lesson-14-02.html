LESSON 14.02 · STEERING WITH WORDS
Two verbs you own, plus a handful of phrases
Where something is = estar. Where you're going = ir a. The directions in between are fixed phrases.
👋 THE TWO JOBS
To move around a city in Spanish, you only need two verbs — and you already have both.

When you say where a place is, use estar (the location verb): El cine está a la derecha — The movie theater is on the right. When you say where you are going, use ir a: Voy a la plaza — I'm going to the plaza. Everything this week hangs on that one split.

🔄 QUICK RECALL · IR A & ESTAR

You met both earlier this year. Here are the forms you'll lean on:

  • Voy a… — I'm going to…  ·  Vamos a… — We're going to…
  • …está aquí / a la derecha — …is here / on the right
  • …está cerca de / lejos de… — …is near / far from…

Notice we say a la plaza (to the plaza) — ir almost always brings its little a along.

📝 THE DIRECTION PHRASES · LEARN THEM WHOLE

These are the phrases people actually say when they point you somewhere. Learn each one as a single fixed chunk — you do not change any endings:

PhrasePictureMeaning
a la derecha➡️to / on the right
a la izquierda⬅️to / on the left
sigue derecho⬆️go / keep straight ahead
dobla a la derecha↪️turn right
dobla a la izquierda↩️turn left
cerca de…📍near…
lejos de…🏞️far from…
💡 A NOTE ON "SIGUE" AND "DOBLA"

Sigue (keep going) and dobla (turn) are a special "telling someone to do something" form of a verb. You'll learn how that form is built later — for now, just grab these as ready-made phrases, the same way you already use por favor or buen provecho. You'll recognize them when you hear them and say them exactly as they are. No endings to change.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Watch the two verbs and the fixed phrases work together. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and say it back:

La biblioteca está a la derecha. — The library is on the right.
Sigue derecho dos cuadras. — Go straight two blocks. (cuadra = block)
Dobla a la izquierda en la esquina. — Turn left at the corner. (esquina = corner)
El parque está cerca de la escuela. — The park is near the school.
Voy a la plaza. — I'm going to the plaza. (ir a)

See the pattern? estar to locate, ir a to go, and the direction phrases dropped in whole.

⚡ TRY IT · FOLLOW THE DIRECTION
Read the Spanish, then choose what it tells you to do or where the place is. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. «Dobla a la izquierda.» What do you do?
2. «Sigue derecho.» What do you do?
3. «La tienda está cerca de la plaza.» The store is…
4. «El cine está a la derecha.» The cinema is on the…
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

Two verbs carry the whole city: estar says where a place is, ir a says where you're going. Drop the fixed direction phrases — a la derecha, a la izquierda, sigue derecho, dobla — in between, and you can steer anyone anywhere.
