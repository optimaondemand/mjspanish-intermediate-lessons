LESSON 14.01 · WELCOME TO THE CITY
This week you find your way around town
Nothing brand-new to conjugate — you already own the verbs. This week you point, you steer, you arrive.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You can already say where you go. This week you learn to say where things are — and how to get there.

Last week you ordered food in a restaurant. This week you step outside into la ciudad (the city). You already know how to say Voy a la plaza (I'm going to the plaza) and La escuela está aquí (The school is here). Now we add the words that let you give and follow directions — left, right, straight ahead, near, far. By Friday you'll be able to walk a friend across town with your words alone.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero. You already carry two tools that this whole week rides on:

ir a… — to go to… (near future / plans) estar — to be (location) Voy a… — I'm going to… …está aquí — …is here

Press ▶ to hear each one — these are review, so let your ear lead:

Voy al cine. — I'm going to the movie theater. (ir a)
La biblioteca está aquí. — The library is here. (estar · location)

Remember the split: ir a says where you're going; estar says where something is. Hold onto that — tomorrow it does all the heavy lifting.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS WEEK YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
name the main parts of a city — la calle, la avenida, la esquina, la cuadra.
2
give and follow simple directions — a la derecha, a la izquierda, sigue derecho, dobla.
3
describe where a place is with estar — cerca de (near) and lejos de (far from).
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
When you're somewhere new and need to find a place, what do you usually do?
💡 WHY THIS IS AN EASY WEEK

Here's the good news: you are not learning new verb endings this week. The directions you'll use — sigue derecho (go straight), dobla a la derecha (turn right) — are fixed phrases. You learn them whole, like buen provecho or por favor, and you never have to change their endings. Everything else rides on two verbs you already own: ir a and estar.

★ A WORD ON COURTESY

Asking for directions is a small act of trust — and giving them is a small act of service. In many Spanish-speaking towns, a stranger will happily walk you half a block to make sure you find your way. Learning to ask kindly, and to help clearly, is courtesy in any language.
