LESSON 14.03 · NEW WORDS, KNOWN GRAMMAR
The map words & the direction words
You already own the grammar (estar, ir a). Today we just load in the words that ride on it.
👋 TWO SMALL FAMILIES OF WORDS

Today's vocabulary comes in two neat groups. First, the parts of a city — the street, the avenue, the corner, the block. Second, the direction phrases from yesterday, all gathered in one place with native audio so you can hear them said correctly. Listen to each, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

🌷 A NOTE ON GENDER

All four of today's city nouns are feminine — they take la (and las in the plural): la calle, la avenida, la esquina, la cuadra. We color feminine words red so your eye learns the gender along with the word.

🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · PARTS OF THE CITY

These are all feminine nouns — press play and say each one aloud with its la.

  • la callethe street
  • la avenida loanword · avenuethe avenue (a wide street)
  • la esquinathe corner
  • la cuadrathe (city) block
🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · DIRECTION PHRASES

These are fixed phrases — no gender, no endings to change. Grab each one whole.

  • a la derechato / on the right
  • a la izquierdato / on the left
  • sigue derechogo / keep straight ahead
  • doblaturn (dobla a la derecha / a la izquierda)
  • cerca denear…
  • lejos defar from…
💡 SPANISH HIDING IN ENGLISH

Two of today's words are hiding in plain sight. English borrowed avenida as avenue, and it uses the Spanish word plaza just as it is — think of a shopping plaza. Tomorrow you'll meet la plaza as something much bigger than a strip mall: the beating heart of a Spanish-speaking city.

📝 PUT THEM TO WORK

Because these ride on grammar you already own, you can build real sentences right now:

  • La tienda está en la esquina. — The store is on the corner.
  • La biblioteca está a dos cuadras. — The library is two blocks away.
  • Vamos por la avenida. — We're going along the avenue.

Same two verbs as always: estar to say where something is, ir a to say where you're headed.
