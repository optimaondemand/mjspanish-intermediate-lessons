👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
You already say what you do. This week you learn to say what you are doing — right this second.

In English there is a difference between “I study Spanish” (in general, every day) and “I am studying Spanish” (right now, at this very moment). Spanish has that same difference — and this week you build the second one: the present progressive. It answers one question: ¿Qué estás haciendo? — What are you doing right now?

Here's the good news: it's built from two pieces you already have. Let's pull them back up.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

Piece one: the verb ESTAR (to be — for feelings and location). You've used it since last year. Read the five forms out loud:

yo estoy — I am tú estás — you are usted/él/ella está — you/he/she is nosotros estamos — we are ustedes/ellos/ellas están — you all/they are

Piece two: the action verbs you've been conjugating for weeks — hablar, comer, estudiar, escribir, leer, correr, cocinar, jugar. You already know what they mean.

Snap those two pieces together and you can say what someone is doing at this moment. That's the whole week — and it's less new than it looks.

💡 TWO KINDS OF “PRESENT”

You already own the first kind. This week you add the second:

  • Simple present → a habit or a general fact: Yo estudio español. — I study Spanish (in general, every day).
  • Present progressive → what's happening at this moment: Yo estoy estudiando español. — I am studying Spanish (right now).

Both are present tense — both are about today, not yesterday. The progressive just zooms in on this exact second. Two little words — ahora (now) and en este momento (at this moment) — are its best friends.

★ A WORD ON ATTENTION

The present progressive is the tense of paying attention to the here and now. Naming what you are actually doing — not what you meant to do, not what's next — is a small act of focus. Do these twenty minutes with full attention, and you'll get more from them than an hour of half-there scrolling.
