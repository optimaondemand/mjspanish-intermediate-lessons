LESSON 12.02 · THE CORE
Facts & know-how vs. people & places
Two verbs, two full paradigms, and one simple question that tells you which to use.
👋 THE ONE QUESTION TO ASK
Am I talking about a fact / a skill, or about being familiar with a person, place, or thing?

saber is for facts, information, and knowing how to do something. If you can picture the information written down — a phone number, an answer, a date — or you can add “how to” (know how to swim), it's saber.

conocer is for being familiar or acquainted with a person, a place, or a thing. If you would say in English “I'm familiar with it” or “I've met them” or “I've been there,” it's conocer.

📝 THE TWO PARADIGMS · PRESENT TENSE

Both verbs are regular except for the yo (I) form — and that one form is the whole trick. The yo forms are highlighted in gold: sé and conozco. Everything else follows the normal endings you already know.

saber — to know (facts / how-to)
yo (I)sé
tú (you, friendly)sabes
usted / él / ellasabe
nosotros / nosotras (we)sabemos
ustedes / ellos / ellassaben
conocer — to know (be familiar with)
yo (I)conozco
tú (you, friendly)conoces
usted / él / ellaconoce
nosotros / nosotras (we)conocemos
ustedes / ellos / ellasconocen

Notice the group form is ustedes (“you all”) — the standard across Latin America, and the form we use all year. Only sé and conozco are surprises; the other four forms of each verb are exactly what you'd expect.

🧩 A SPECIAL POWER · SABER + INFINITIVE

Put saber right in front of another verb's infinitive (the “to ___” form) and it means “to know how to” do that thing:

  • Sé nadar. — I know how to swim.
  • Ella sabe cocinar. — She knows how to cook.
  • Nosotros sabemos hablar español. — We know how to speak Spanish.

You don't add any word for “how” — saber + the plain infinitive already carries it.

💡 HEAR THE SPLIT

Press ▶ and say each one back — two for saber, two for conocer:

Yo sé tu número de teléfono. — I know your phone number. (a fact)
Yo sé tocar la guitarra. — I know how to play the guitar. (a skill)
Yo conozco a tu hermana. — I know your sister. (a person)
Yo conozco la ciudad de Miami. — I know the city of Miami. (a place)
👉 ONE SMALL EXTRA · THE PERSONAL A

Did you spot the little a in Conozco a tu hermana? Whenever the thing you “know” is a specific person, Spanish puts an a right before them. It has no English translation — it's just a signpost that says “a real person is coming.” A place gets no a: Conozco Miami. You'll practice this fully on Day 4 — for now, just notice it.

⚡ TRY IT · WHICH VERB — SABER OR CONOCER?
Ask yourself: fact/skill, or familiar-with a person/place? Pick the verb that fits. You'll get instant feedback on each one.
1. Yo ____ la respuesta correcta. (I know the correct answer.)
2. Nosotros ____ la ciudad de Madrid. (We know the city of Madrid.)
3. ¿____ (tú) nadar? (Do you know how to swim?)
4. Ustedes ____ a la directora. (You all know the principal.)
5. Yo no ____ su dirección. (I don't know his address.)
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

saber = facts, information, know-how (yo → sé). conocer = familiar with a person, place, or thing (yo → conozco). Only the yo forms are irregular. And a specific person gets a personal a in front.
