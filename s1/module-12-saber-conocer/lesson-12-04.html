LESSON 12.04 · IN THE REAL WORLD
Who & what you know — and that little a
See both verbs working in a real paragraph, meet some famous names, and lock in the personal a.
📖 READ & LISTEN · “LO QUE SÉ Y A QUIÉN CONOZCO”

Here is Daniela talking about what she knows and who she knows. Press ▶ to hear each Spanish sentence, then read the English underneath:

Yo sé muchas cosas sobre el fútbol. — I know many things about soccer.
También sé jugar al fútbol. — I also know how to play soccer.
Conozco a muchos futbolistas famosos. — I know (of) many famous soccer players.
Mi hermana conoce la ciudad de Barcelona. — My sister knows the city of Barcelona.
Ella sabe hablar español y también sabe cantar. — She knows how to speak Spanish and also knows how to sing.
Nosotros conocemos a Shakira, la cantante de Colombia. — We know (of) Shakira, the singer from Colombia.

Look at the pattern: sé and sabe carry facts and skills; conozco, conoce, and conocemos carry people and places. And every time a person follows, there's that little a — but never before a place (la ciudad de Barcelona, no a).

👉 THE RULE · THE PERSONAL A

Here is the whole rule in one line:

When the thing receiving the action is a specific person, put a right before them.
  • Conozco a María. — I know María. (person → a)
  • Veo a mi amigo. — I see my friend. (person → a, even with other verbs)
  • Conozco Madrid. — I know Madrid. (place → no a)
  • Sé la respuesta. — I know the answer. (thing/fact → no a)

The personal a has no English translation — you just don't say it in English. It's a little Spanish signpost meaning “a real person is the object here.”

🌐 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · FAMOUS FIGURES YOU ALREADY “KNOW”

You can use conocer for famous people the way English says “I know of them” — you're familiar with who they are. Chances are you already “know” several figures from the Spanish-speaking world:

Lionel Messi
Argentine futbolista (soccer player), one of the most famous athletes alive.
Shakira
Colombian cantante (singer) known around the world.
Frida Kahlo
Mexican artista (painter), famous for her self-portraits.
Miguel de Cervantes
Spanish escritor (writer) who wrote Don Quijote, one of the most famous novels ever written.
Rafael Nadal
Spanish tennis champion — a name you can “know” even if you've never met him.

Try it in your head: ¿Conoces a Shakira? — Do you know (of) Shakira? Notice the personal a, because Shakira is a person.

⚡ TRY IT · VERB CHOICE & THE PERSONAL A
Pick the word that completes each sentence. Watch for people (they take a) vs. places and facts (they don't). Instant feedback on each.
1. Conozco ____ Frida Kahlo. (I know [of] Frida Kahlo.)
2. Conozco ____ el museo de la ciudad. (I know the city's museum.)
3. Yo ____ a la profesora Martínez. (I know Ms. Martínez.)
4. ¿____ (tú) qué hora es? (Do you know what time it is?)
🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

In real Spanish, saber and conocer mix freely in the same paragraph — you just keep asking “fact/skill or familiar-with?” And whenever a specific person is the object, drop in the personal a.
