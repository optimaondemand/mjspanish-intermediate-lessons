LESSON 12.03 · THINGS TO KNOW
The people, places & skills you can “know”
Known grammar, new vocabulary — today is words, so the only job is to listen and repeat.
👋 WHY THESE WORDS, THIS WEEK

You already own the grammar from yesterday. Today we load in the vocabulary that rides on it — three little families that fit saber and conocer perfectly: people you're acquainted with, places you're familiar with, and skills you know how to do. Listen to each word in a native voice, then use the flip cards to test yourself.

Blue = masculine nounRed = feminine nounSkills are verbs (infinitives) — no gender.
👤 LISTEN & REPEAT · LAS PERSONAS (PEOPLE — use with conocer)

People-words change form and article by sex: color shows the gender.

  • la personathe person (always feminine, even for a man)
  • el vecino / la vecinathe neighbor (m / f)
  • el / la cantantethe singer (m / f)
  • el escritor / la escritorathe writer (m / f)
  • el / la artistathe artist (m / f)
  • el / la futbolistathe soccer player (m / f)
🏙️ LISTEN & REPEAT · LOS LUGARES (PLACES — use with conocer)
  • la ciudadthe city
  • el paísthe country
  • el museothe museum
  • el estadiothe stadium
🏅 LISTEN & REPEAT · LAS HABILIDADES (SKILLS — use with saber + infinitive)

These are infinitives (“to ___”). Put saber in front to say you know how to do them: Sé nadar.

  • nadarto swim
  • cantarto sing
  • bailarto dance
  • tocar la guitarrato play the guitar
💡 A NICE PAIR

Notice cantar (to sing) and el/la cantante (singer) share a root — a person who sings is a singer. Spanish builds people-words from action-words all the time, so learning one often gives you the other for free.

📝 PUT THEM TO WORK

With yesterday's verbs, you can already build real sentences:

  • Yo conozco a mi vecino. — I know my neighbor. (person → conocer + a)
  • Nosotros conocemos la ciudad. — We know the city. (place → conocer)
  • Ella sabe bailar. — She knows how to dance. (skill → saber + infinitive)

Tomorrow you'll read a short passage that uses all three at once.
