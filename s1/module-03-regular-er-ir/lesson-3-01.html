👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
Spanish has exactly three verb families: -AR, -ER, and -IR. You conquered the first one last week — the other two are cousins of it.

Welcome to Module 3. Last week you learned to conjugate regular -AR verbs: drop the ending, add the ending for the subject. This week you meet the two remaining families, -ER and -IR. Here's the good news: they follow the same three steps as -AR, and their endings are nearly identical to each other. Today is just a look at how little is actually new.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

You are not starting from zero — you finished Module 2 owning the whole -AR pattern. Say these out loud; you built every one:

hablo — I speak hablas — you speak habla — he/she speaks hablamos — we speak hablan — they speak

That's the -AR chant: -o / -as / -a / -amos / -an. Keep it in your ear — this week you'll see how much of it carries straight over to -ER and -IR. (We still use ustedes for a group, all year long.)

📚 A 30-SECOND PEEK · HOW CLOSE THEY REALLY ARE

Look how much the three families share. Every ending below matches across -ER and -IR except one:

  • yo, tú, él/ella, and the group forms are the same for -ER and -IR: -o / -es / -e / … / -en.
  • The only place they differ is nosotros ("we"): -ER uses -emos (comemos), -IR uses -imos (vivimos).

Press ▶ to hear the difference in the "we" form — that one tiny swap is the whole week:

Nosotros comemos. — We eat. (-ER → -emos)
Nosotros vivimos. — We live. (-IR → -imos)

Don't worry about memorizing it now — that's tomorrow's job. Today, just notice how small the leap is.
