LESSON 3.04 · SEE THE PATTERN AT WORK
A real day, in -ER and -IR verbs
Read a short paragraph you can actually understand, then meet the Spanish verbs hiding inside English.
📖 READ · EL DÍA DE MARCOS

Every verb below is a regular -ER or -IR verb in the present tense. Read it once for the gist, then again slowly. Press ▶ to hear each sentence and read along.

Marcos vive en Texas con su familia. — Marcos lives in Texas with his family.
Por la mañana, él come el desayuno y bebe leche. — In the morning, he eats breakfast and drinks milk.
En la escuela, Marcos y sus amigos leen y aprenden mucho. — At school, Marcos and his friends read and learn a lot.
Marcos comprende bien la lección y escribe en su cuaderno. — Marcos understands the lesson well and writes in his notebook.
Por la tarde, él recibe un mensaje y decide practicar el piano. — In the afternoon, he receives a message and decides to practice the piano.
En casa, Marcos abre su libro y estudia español. — At home, Marcos opens his book and studies Spanish.
WORD HELP

Notice how vive, come, bebe, comprende, escribe, recibe, and abre all use the -e ending (he = one person), while leen and aprenden use -en (Marcos and his friends = they). The endings do the work of showing who.

🌐 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · COGNATE VERBS

Here's a gift English and Spanish share: cognate verbs — verbs that look alike and mean the same thing in both languages, because both inherited them from Latin. Several of this week's -ER/-IR verbs hide inside English words you already use:

comprender→ comprehend (to understand)
decidir→ decide
recibir→ receive (think of a recipe or a receipt)
describir→ describe (a cousin of escribir, "to write")

Once you see the pattern, dozens of Spanish verbs stop looking foreign. When you meet an -ER or -IR verb that echoes an English word — and it usually does — guess boldly. The check below mixes a couple of reading questions with these cognate links.

⚡ TRY IT · READING + COGNATE CHECK
A few questions about Marcos's day and the cognate verbs. Pick one per row for instant feedback — read the options carefully, the order changes.
1. Where does Marcos live?
2. What does Marcos drink in the morning?
3. The verb comprender is a cognate of which English word?
4. What does recibir mean?
5. In the afternoon, Marcos decide to… (decidir = ?)
💡 THE STRATEGY

You just read a whole paragraph of Spanish and understood it — because you know the -ER/-IR endings and because cognates handed you half the vocabulary for free. Reading and hearing a sentence together (that's what the ▶ buttons are for) is one of the fastest ways to make new verbs stick.
