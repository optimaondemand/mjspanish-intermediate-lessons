LESSON 17.02 · THREE LITTLE TOOLS
Almost all weather uses just three patterns
Learn which tool goes with which kind of weather, and you can describe nearly any sky.
👋 THE ONE IDEA
Spanish doesn't say "it is sunny." It says, roughly, "it makes sun." Weather leans on the verb hacer.

To ask about the weather you say ¿Qué tiempo hace? — literally "What weather does it make?" To answer, you pick one of three tools. Which tool you use depends on the kind of weather — and there's a simple reason behind each one.

🌞 TOOL 1 · hace + a noun

Most weather uses hace + a naming word. Hace is just the he/she/it form of hacer (to make/do) — the same verb you met with the "yo-go" verbs. Because the weather is an "it," you only ever need this one form: hace.

SpanishEnglish
Hace sol.It's sunny. (lit. "it makes sun")
Hace calor.It's hot.
Hace frío.It's cold.
Hace viento.It's windy.
Hace fresco.It's cool.
Hace buen tiempo.The weather is nice.
Hace mal tiempo.The weather is bad.

One form fits them all: hace + word. You can even stack it: Hace sol y hace calor. — It's sunny and hot.

⛅ TOOL 2 · está + a describing word

For the state of the sky, Spanish uses está — the he/she/it form of estar, the "to be" verb you already know for conditions. The sky is in a temporary state, so estar is the natural fit.

SpanishEnglish
Está nublado.It's cloudy.
Está despejado.It's clear.

Careful: cloudy and clear take está, not hace. Say Está nublado — never Hace nublado.

🌧️ TOOL 3 · the two weather verbs

Rain and snow get their own verbs. Here's the friendly part: only the sky rains or snows, so each verb has exactly one form — nothing to conjugate.

VerbThe one formMeaning
llover (o→ue)llueveit rains / it's raining
nevar (e→ie)nievait snows / it's snowing

Notice the "boot" stem-changes you already know: llover is an o→ue verb (llover → llueve) and nevar is an e→ie verb (nevar → nieva). Same idea as poder and querer — just used with only one subject: the weather.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Each sentence uses one of the three tools. Press ▶ to hear it and say it back:

Hoy hace sol. — It's sunny today. (tool 1: hace)
Hace mucho frío hoy. — It's very cold today. (tool 1: hace)
En la mañana está nublado. — In the morning it's cloudy. (tool 2: está)
Llueve mucho en abril. — It rains a lot in April. (tool 3: verb)
Nieva en las montañas. — It snows in the mountains. (tool 3: verb)

Pick the tool that fits: hace for most weather, está for the sky's state, and llueve / nieva for rain and snow.

🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

Three tools cover the sky: hace + noun (sol, calor, frío, viento, fresco, buen/mal tiempo), está + word (nublado, despejado), and the two one-form verbs llueve and nieva. Ask it all with ¿Qué tiempo hace?
