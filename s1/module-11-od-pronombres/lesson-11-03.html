LESSON 11.03 · KNOWN WORDS, ONE JOB
Ten nouns — and the pronoun each one becomes
No new grammar today. These are words you already own; we just tag each one with its gender, because gender decides the pronoun.
👋 WHY GENDER IS THE WHOLE GAME TODAY

Yesterday you learned the two rules: match and place. Matching depends entirely on one thing you already know for each noun — is it masculine (el) or feminine (la)? So today we bring back ten everyday nouns and lock in their gender, because that's what tells you whether to reach for lo/los or la/las.

masculine → lo (one) / los (many) feminine → la (one) / las (many)
🔊 LISTEN & REPEAT · TEN NOUNS YOU CAN REPLACE

Press play and say each one aloud. The color tells you the gender: blue = masculine, red = feminine.

  • el librothe book · → lo
  • el diccionariothe dictionary · → lo
  • el parquethe park · → lo
  • el restaurantethe restaurant · → lo
  • el españolSpanish (the language) · → lo
  • la computadorathe computer · → la
  • la músicathe music · → la
  • la cenathe dinner · → la
  • la casathe house · → la
  • la tiendathe store · → la
💡 ONE STEP TO PLURAL

Make the noun plural and the pronoun goes plural too: el libro → los libros → los;   la computadora → las computadoras → las. Gender picks lo/la; number picks whether you add the -s.

📝 PUT ONE TO WORK

Now that each noun has its gender tagged, the pronoun is automatic:

  • ¿El diccionario? — Sí, lo uso. — The dictionary? Yes, I use it.
  • ¿La música? — Sí, la escucho. — Music? Yes, I listen to it.
  • ¿Las tiendas? — Sí, las visito. — The stores? Yes, I visit them.

The verb stays exactly as you learned it — only the little pronoun is new.
