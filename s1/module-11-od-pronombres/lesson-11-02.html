LESSON 11.02 · THE PATTERN
Match the noun, then place the word
Two small rules run every direct-object pronoun. Learn them once and they work everywhere.
👋 FIRST · WHAT IS A DIRECT OBJECT?
The direct object is the noun that receives the action — the answer to “what?” or “whom?”

In Yo leo el libro (“I read the book”), ask read what? → el libro. That's the direct object. A direct-object pronoun is the little word that takes its place so you don't repeat it: Yo lo leo — “I read it.” Same move you make in English every day.

🧩 THE TWO RULES
1. Match the noun's gender & number + 2. Place it before the verb

Rule 1: pick the pronoun that agrees with the noun you're replacing — el libro (masculine, singular) → lo. Rule 2: drop that pronoun in right before the conjugated verb — Lo leo. That's the everyday pattern. (One extra placement option comes up below.)

📝 THE SEVEN PRONOUNS
Replaces…PronounExample (present tense)
memeElla me escucha. — She listens to me.
you (friendly)teYo te comprendo. — I understand you.
it (masc.) / himlo¿El libro? Lo tengo. — The book? I have it.
it (fem.) / herla¿La cena? La preparo. — Dinner? I make it.

them (masc.)los¿Los libros? Los leo. — The books? I read them.
them (fem.)las¿Las computadoras? Las uso. — The computers? I use them.

me, te, and nos never change — they're always about people (me / you / us). Only lo / la / los / las shift for gender and number, because they stand in for things that carry gender. Notice the group form is always ustedes / ellos / ellas across Latin America — there is no separate “you-all” object word beyond los / las.

📌 WHERE THE PRONOUN GOES

There are two correct spots. Most of the time you'll use the first:

  • Before the conjugated verb (the everyday spot): Lo leo. — I read it.   La uso. — I use it.
  • Attached to the end of an infinitive: when there's a verb + infinitive (like ir a + infinitive from Module 9), you may hook the pronoun onto the infinitive: Voy a leerlo. — I'm going to read it.

Both of these are correct and mean the same thing: Lo voy a leer.  =  Voy a leerlo. What you cannot do is leave the pronoun floating loose in the middle: ✗ Voy a lo leer.

💡 SEE IT IN REAL SENTENCES

Watch a full noun get swapped for its pronoun. Press ▶ to hear each one and say it back:

¿Tienes el libro? — Sí, lo tengo. (el libro → lo)
Yo uso la computadora. → La uso. (la computadora → la)
Ella lee los libros. → Los lee. (los libros → los)
Nosotros escuchamos la música. → La escuchamos. (la música → la)
¿Vas a leer el libro? — Sí, voy a leerlo. (attached to the infinitive)

Every time: find the direct object, note its gender and number, pick the matching pronoun, and drop it before the verb (or hook it onto the infinitive).

🔑 TODAY'S TAKEAWAY

Two rules: match the noun (lo / la / los / las for things; me / te / nos for people) and place the pronoun before the verb — or attached to an infinitive. That's every direct-object pronoun you'll use this year.
