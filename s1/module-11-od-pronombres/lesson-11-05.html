LESSON 11.05 · BRING IT TOGETHER
You can replace a noun with one small word
A quick recap, an honest self-check, and then the short quiz that unlocks Module 12.
🔑 THE WEEK IN ONE SCREEN

Two rules run everything:

  • Match — the pronoun copies the noun's gender and number.
  • Place — put it before the conjugated verb (Lo leo), or attach it to an infinitive (Voy a leerlo). Never loose in the middle.

The seven pronouns — blue for things that are masculine, red for feminine, gold for people:

lo — it (masc.) / him los — them (masc.) la — it (fem.) / her las — them (fem.) me — me te — you nos — us

And remember the register you use all year: for a group it's always ustedes / ellos / ellas — there is no vosotros form here.

⚡ SELF-CHECK · CAN YOU PICK THE RIGHT LITTLE WORD?
Five mixed questions from the whole week. Pick one for each — you'll get instant feedback. This is practice; the graded version is the quick-check below.
1. ¿Tienes el diccionario? — Sí, ____ tengo.
2. Yo escucho la música. → ____ escucho.
3. ¿Lees los libros? — Sí, ____ leo.
4. How do you say “I understand you”? — Yo ____ comprendo.
5. How do you say “I'm going to use it” (la computadora)?
✅ BEFORE THE QUIZ · YOU CAN NOW…
  • ✓
    find the direct object in a sentence — the noun that receives the action.
  • ✓
    choose lo / los for masculine and la / las for feminine, matching gender and number.
  • ✓
    use me / te / nos when the object is a person.
  • ✓
    place the pronoun before the verb or attached to an infinitive.

If any of those feels shaky, replay lesson 11.02's drill or 11.04's reading before you start — the quick-check gives you unlimited attempts, so it's there to help you learn, not to trip you.

Module 12 — Saber vs. Conocer
Spanish has two verbs for “to know” — one for facts and skills, one for people and places — plus the little “personal a” you use before a person. You'll build on exactly the pronoun-and-people idea you just learned.
★ A WORD ON PERSEVERANCE

Small words are easy to skim past and surprisingly worth mastering. Getting lo vs. la right, every time, is the kind of quiet precision that separates “I studied Spanish” from “I can actually speak it.” Keep showing up for the small things — that's perseverance, and it's how fluency is really built.
