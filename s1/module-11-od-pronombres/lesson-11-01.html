LESSON 11.01 · STOP REPEATING YOURSELF
One little word can replace the whole noun
Nothing new to memorize today — just notice the problem this week solves, and warm up the words you already own.
👋 TODAY'S BIG IDEA
In English you don't say “I have the book, I read the book, I love the book.” You say “I have it, I read it, I love it.” Spanish does the same thing — with one small word.

Listen to how repetitive this sounds: “¿El libro? Sí, tengo el libro. Leo el libro por la noche.” Nobody talks like that. This week you learn the little words that let you say Lo tengo and Lo leo instead — the direct-object pronouns. They replace a noun you've already mentioned, so you don't have to keep repeating it. You already do this every day in English without thinking about it.

🔄 FIRST · WHAT YOU ALREADY OWN

This week hangs on two things you already have: verbs you can conjugate and nouns with a gender. Read these out loud — every one is review:

Yo tengo… — I have… Yo leo… — I read… Nosotros escuchamos… — We listen to… Ella usa… — She uses…

And the nouns those verbs act on — notice each one is either masculine (el) or feminine (la). That gender is going to matter a lot this week:

el libro la computadora la música la cena el diccionario

The direct-object pronoun you pick has to match the gender and number of the noun it replaces. That's the one new idea — and you already know the genders.

🎯 BY THE END OF THIS WEEK YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
spot the direct object — the noun receiving the action — in a sentence.
2
choose the right pronoun — lo, la, los, las (plus me, te, nos) — so it agrees with the noun.
3
place the pronoun correctly — before the verb (Lo leo) or attached to an infinitive (Voy a leerlo).
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Think about English for a second: “Do you have the keys? Yes, I have them.” How does swapping a noun for a little word like it or them feel to you?
👀 A QUICK PEEK · THE MOVE YOU'LL LEARN

Here's the whole trick in one picture — you'll unpack it fully tomorrow:

  • ¿Tienes el libro? — Sí, lo tengo.  (el libro → lo)
  • Yo uso la computadora. → La uso.  (la computadora → la)

See how the pronoun copies the noun's gender? el libro → lo, la computadora → la. That match-up is the heart of the week.

★ A WORD ON COURTESY

Little words carry a lot of care. When you say “Te comprendo” — “I understand you” — that one tiny pronoun te tells another person they were heard. Learning to aim these words at the right listener is a small act of courtesy: it's language paying attention to the people in front of it.
