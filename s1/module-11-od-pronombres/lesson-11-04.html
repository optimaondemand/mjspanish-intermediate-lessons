LESSON 11.04 · SEE THEM IN THE WILD
Un día en casa — A day at home
Watch how a real paragraph leans on these little pronouns so it never repeats a noun twice.
👋 READ, THEN LISTEN

Here's a short paragraph in everyday present-tense Spanish — every verb and noun is one you already know. As you read, notice each direct-object pronoun (in bold) and ask yourself: which noun does it replace? Press ▶ on each line to hear it read in a native voice.

📚 LA LECTURA · THE READING
Yo tengo muchos libros en mi casa. — I have many books in my house.
Los leo por la noche. — I read them at night. (los = los libros)
Mi hermano tiene una computadora nueva. — My brother has a new computer.
La usa para estudiar el español. — He uses it to study Spanish. (la = la computadora)
Mi mamá prepara la cena y nosotros la comemos juntos. — My mom makes dinner and we eat it together. (la = la cena)
A mí me gusta la música; la escucho todos los días. — I like music; I listen to it every day. (la = la música)

Notice: not one noun is repeated after its first mention. Each pronoun quietly points back to the noun that came before it — and each one matches that noun's gender and number.

🌎 SPANISH IN YOUR WORLD · A VERB HIDING IN ENGLISH

Some Spanish verbs are cognates — you can read them because their English twin is right there inside the word. comprender hides in comprehend, decidir in decide, describir in describe. When the thing these verbs act on is a person, you reach for the people-pronouns from this week:

Yo te comprendo. — I understand you.   Ellos nos comprenden. — They understand us.

So lo / la / los / las replace things, while me / te / nos point at people — and a cognate verb makes the sentence easy to read from the very first try.
